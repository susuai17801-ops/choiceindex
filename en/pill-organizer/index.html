Pill Organizer in Taiwan: How to Choose for Small Pills and Large Supplements, Plus Official Medicine Storage Reminders (藥盒)

Last updated: November 15, 2026

Medicine or supplements you take every day are easier to carry and keep track of when you sort them into a pill organizer in advance. Pill organizers look alike, but they differ in how big the compartments are, how tight the lid is, and how many days they hold. This page covers five things to check, then recommends two products: the Westwood sealed silicone 7-day box for small pills, and the Fullicon (護立康) 14-compartment moisture-resistant box for large supplements (per the sellers).

Quick Checklist Before You Buy

  • How many times a day? → Once a day, 7 or 14 compartments is enough; several times a day, look at multi-slot boxes such as 28 compartments
  • How big is the largest piece? → Compare with the box size on the product page and ask the seller for compartment size
  • What will go in? → For medicine, read the Ministry of Health and Welfare reminder first (medicine that easily absorbs moisture should not be transferred to a pill box); if unsure, ask a pharmacist
  • Is the box dry? → Clean it before first use and fill it only once fully dry
  • How do warranty and returns work? → Always follow the store page; save a screenshot before ordering

Official Sources: Reminders on Splitting and Storing Medicine in Taiwan

  • What to watch when splitting medicine — The Ministry of Health and Welfare (衛生福利部) article “藥品存放多留意，確保療效又安心” says some people move medicine into a pill box after picking it up, and that medicine which easily absorbs moisture and breaks down should not be split this way (Ministry page, filed August 24, 2011, read November 15, 2026).
  • Original packaging and leaflets — The same source says medicine should stay in its original packaging, oral and external medicines should be kept apart, and the leaflet and original packaging should be kept to check the expiry date and directions. Issue 1009 (January 17, 2025) of the Food and Drug Administration's (食藥署) Drug and Food Safety Weekly also says to keep the original packaging and leaflet.
  • Where to store it — The Ministry source says medicine is best kept somewhere dry and cool, such as a drawer in the dark, and not somewhere humid, stuffy, or in direct sunlight, such as the bathroom, kitchen, or by a window.
  • When to throw it away — The Food and Drug Administration weekly says medicine that has passed its expiry date, or shows deterioration such as deliquescing, crumbling, deforming, or changing color, should be discarded immediately.
  • What we did not read — We did not read an official rule on the maximum number of days of medicine you may prepare at once, official advice on splitting supplements, or whether pill boxes count as medical devices or inspected goods. The Ministry page above was published long ago; for current content, follow the agency's latest information or a pharmacist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a moisture-resistant pill box hold any medicine?
Not necessarily. Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare says medicine that easily absorbs moisture and breaks down should not be split into pill boxes. A moisture-resistant box only reduces outside air getting in; it does not replace the original packaging. If unsure, ask a pharmacist.
Can small pills and large supplements share one box?
We did not find official advice on mixing medicine and supplements. We recommend two products because the compartment sizes differ: the 7-compartment box for small pills and the 14-compartment box for large capsules or tablets. Ask a doctor or pharmacist what can go in.
How many days of medicine can one box hold?
The two recommended products have 7 compartments (one week) and 14 compartments (two weeks) per the sellers. As for the maximum number of days, we did not find a clear official rule; ask a doctor or pharmacist.
Should I wash the box first?
The Fullicon seller says to clean it and let it dry completely before first use; the Westwood page does not say. Neither page states heat resistance or how to wash it, and we did not test them.
