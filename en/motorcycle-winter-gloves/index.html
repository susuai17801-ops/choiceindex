How to Choose Winter Motorcycle Gloves in Taiwan: Warmth, Wind and Water Resistance, Dexterity, Touchscreen, Size

Last updated: November 9, 2026

When you ride in winter, your hands are usually the first part of your body to get cold. Winter gloves come as fleece-lined, wind- and water-resistant, and hard-shell protective types, with different thicknesses and levels of dexterity, so choosing the right pair can be confusing. You can look at six things: warmth and thickness; wind and water resistance; dexterity and grip; touchscreen compatibility; size and hand shape; warranty and returns. This page goes through them in order, then recommends one we found: a fleece-lined winter riding glove (waterproof, touchscreen) on Shopee. The seller states a split-finger cut, anti-slip texture on the palm and fingertips, and touchscreen thumb and index fingers; it is a general riding and outdoor glove, not a motorcycle glove with protective armor.

Types of Winter Motorcycle Gloves

TypeWording stores often useWhat to confirm before ordering
Fleece-lined, wind-resistantStores often write “加絨” (fleece-lined), “防風” (windproof), “保暖” (warm)How thick the fleece is; whether it is so thick that gripping the handlebar or squeezing the brake lever gets harder
Wind- and water-resistantStores often write “防水” (waterproof), “防潑水” (splash-resistant), “防風” (windproof)Whether the whole layer is waterproof or only the surface repels splashes; whether the cuff tightens
Armored / hard-shellStores often write “護具” (armor), “硬殼” (hard shell), “防摔” (anti-fall)Whether the armor material and rating are stated; usually thicker, so check dexterity and warmth separately

No type is absolutely better; choose by commute distance, weather, and how the glove feels. We have not tested each model; the terms above are commonly used by stores.

What We Found from Official Sources

  • No mandatory rule or dedicated test standard for gloves found — In an internet search, we found no Taiwanese rule requiring motorcycle riders to wear gloves, and no dedicated test standard for motorcycle gloves (we read only search summaries, not the standard text); one site treats gloves as recommended gear. So the points on this page come from wording stores commonly use and general use situations, not an official standard.
  • Touchscreen and Article 31-1 — We read the original text of Article 31-1 of the 《道路交通管理處罰條例》 (Act Governing the Punishment of Violations of Road Traffic Regulations) (November 2, 2026): a motorcycle rider who uses a mobile phone or similar device by holding it in the hand on the road is fined NT$1,200; the text says “by hand”. Whether using a phone with touchscreen gloves while riding is a violation, we found no official interpretation, so we make no judgment and do not cover phone use while riding.
  • What we did not test — We did not test the seller's waterproofing, wind resistance, fleece warmth, anti-slip texture, touchscreen sensitivity, or wash durability; the low-temperature figures and improvement percentages in the seller's copy are also unverified.
  • We do not cover how to ride — This page only organizes buying directions and is not safety advice. The seller's page does not say whether glove thickness affects how you feel the handlebar, brake, and throttle, and we have not tested this; for use, follow the seller's notes.
