Bringing a hair dryer from home to Taiwan, or thinking of buying a new one here? Taiwan's voltage is different from many countries, and a hair dryer draws a lot of power, so buying or plugging in the wrong one can cause problems. This page explains what voltage Taiwan uses and how to read voltage and frequency labels, then what to check when buying a hair dryer sold in Taiwan, and finally recommends one hair dryer that the seller lists as 110V.

## Power in Taiwan: 110V, 60Hz

Under the Ministry of Economic Affairs' Standard for Supply Voltage and Frequency of Electric Utilities (電業供電電壓及頻率標準, amended June 12, 2017), the AC frequency in Taiwan is 60 Hz, and supply methods include 110 volt single-phase two-wire and 110/220 volt single-phase three-wire. Household sockets are usually 110 volts, while air conditioners and other large appliances often have a separate 220 volt socket. We do not have official household-by-household data, so confirm the voltage of your own socket from the label by the socket or from your landlord or a licensed electrician.

## Before Buying, Read the Appliance Label

The body or manual of a hair dryer has a line showing rated voltage, frequency, and power. Check these three numbers first. The table below shows common labels and our suggestion:

| Label | What it means | What to do in Taiwan |
|---|---|---|
| 110V 60Hz | 110 volts, 60 Hz | Matches Taiwan's supply standard; the simplest choice |
| 220V–240V (only this) | Only for a 220–240 volt supply | Usually not the voltage of an ordinary Taiwan socket; do not plug it in directly; we suggest buying a 110V model |
| 110/220V or 110-120/220-240V | Dual voltage; adjust per the manual before use | Under the BSMI inspection standard, appliances marked with different voltages must have instructions on how to adjust them; if the manual does not explain it, do not use it yet |
| 50/60Hz | Works at both 50 and 60 Hz | Taiwan is 60 Hz, so it matches |
| 1200W | 1200 watts of power | At 110V the current is about 11 amps (1200÷110, our rough calculation); we do not recommend plug adapters for high-power appliances, and if you use an extension cord, check its rating first |

A plug adapter only changes the shape of the plug, not the voltage. For a high-power heating appliance like a hair dryer, we do not recommend trying to step up the voltage or using an adapter yourself; buying a 110V model in Taiwan is the simplest. For how to choose adapters and extension cords, see our plug adapter guide and extension cord guide.

## Can a 220V Hair Dryer from Home Be Used?

Look at the label on the body first. A hair dryer marked only 220V–240V uses a voltage different from an ordinary Taiwan socket, so we do not recommend plugging it in directly; when the voltage does not match, the appliance may not work properly or may be damaged (we did not find an official explanation or a test; this is general electrical knowledge, not statistics). If the label shows dual voltage (for example 110/220V), you can set it to 110V following the manual. If you are unsure, take it to an electronics store and ask, or simply buy a 110V one in Taiwan, which is usually less trouble.

## What to Check When Buying a Hair Dryer Sold in Taiwan

- **Commodity inspection mark** — According to Taiwan's BSMI (Bureau of Standards, Metrology and Inspection), hair dryers (rated 250V or below, not for animals) are electrical products subject to mandatory inspection, under standards such as CNS 60335-1 and CNS 60335-2-23. When buying, check for a commodity inspection mark on the body or packaging. The seller of our pick lists BSMI number R31417; we did not verify it.
- **Voltage label** — Hair dryers sold in Taiwan are usually marked 110V 60Hz, but dual-voltage models such as 110/220V also exist. Make sure it says 110V, and for dual-voltage models make sure the manual explains how to switch.
- **Chinese labeling and warnings** — In the BSMI sample test, Chinese labeling and the warnings in the manual were also checked. When it arrives, read the voltage and power on the body first, then the manual.
- **BSMI sample test results** — BSMI tested 10 hair dryers bought from the market in 2018 (year 107 in the Taiwan calendar): temperature rise, leakage current, insulation voltage withstand, abnormal operation, and construction checks all passed; electromagnetic compatibility failed for 3, key component comparison failed for 2, and Chinese labeling failed for 1, while all 10 had the commodity inspection mark. This is a result from several years ago and does not mean every unit today passes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use a 220V hair dryer from home in Taiwan?**
Look at the label on the body first. One marked only 220V–240V uses a voltage different from an ordinary Taiwan socket, so we do not recommend plugging it in directly; one marked 110/220V (dual voltage) can be set to 110V following the manual. A plug adapter only changes the plug shape, not the voltage. We did not find an official explanation or a test; if unsure, ask an electronics store or a licensed electrician.

**How do I switch a 110/220V hair dryer?**
Follow the manual. The BSMI Tainan Branch's summary of the CNS 60335-1 standard says an appliance marked with different rated voltages or frequencies must have instructions on how to adjust it in the manual; if there are none, do not plug it in yet.

**Can I plug a 1200W hair dryer into an extension cord?**
At 110V, 1200W is about 11 amps (our rough calculation). For an extension cord, check its current rating, and we do not recommend adapters for high-power appliances; see our extension cord guide and plug adapter guide.

**Why check the commodity inspection mark?**
According to BSMI, hair dryers are subject to mandatory inspection. The mark shows the product was inspected as required, but sample tests have also found mismatched labels and components, so still check the label and manual when it arrives.
