# How to Choose a Motorcycle Raincoat (機車雨衣) in Taiwan: Two-Piece vs. One-Piece, Material, Reflective Strips, and Breathability

Last updated: October 23, 2026
選品索引 ChoiceIndex — https://choiceindex.tw/en/

If you ride every day to commute, deliver food, or work as a designated driver, the worst things on a rainy day are a raincoat that still lets the water in, one that is so stuffy you sweat, or rain pants that wear through at the seat after sitting for a long time. Motorcycle raincoats look much the same, but the style, material, hood, cuffs, reflective strips, and size differ quite a bit. This guide puts it in a form you can compare directly, so you can see the main points first and then decide which one to buy.

## First, Understand the Common Styles of Motorcycle Raincoat

| Style | Features | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Two-piece (jacket + rain pants) | You can put on and take off the top and bottom separately and wash them separately, and you have more freedom to move while sitting on the bike. Watch for water getting in where the jacket hem overlaps the waist of the pants | Long hours of riding such as daily commuting, delivery, and driver work |
| One-piece (coverall) | It covers a larger area, but is harder to put on and take off, and is also hotter | Occasional rain, or when you need to be covered from head to toe |
| Poncho / cape | The quickest to put on and take off, but the hem is easily blown up in a side wind and the legs are less protected | Short trips, or quickly putting it on at a temporary stop |

No single style is "the best". It depends on how long you ride in a day, how often you get on and off the bike, and how often you ride in the rain. People who ride often and for a long time generally look at the two-piece first.

## 5 Things to Check When Choosing a Motorcycle Raincoat

| What to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Material and seams | There are two main groups: coated fabric (such as PVC-coated nylon) and translucent film (such as PEVA or EVA). Coated fabric is usually more hard-wearing and heavier. Translucent film is usually lighter but wears out easily. If the seams are labeled "fully taped" (全壓膠), the seam lines are sealed with tape to reduce the chance of water seeping through them |
| Breathability | On a long ride it is easy to sweat. Look for a mesh lining, a vented back, or vent holes. Waterproofing and breathability usually involve a trade-off, so do not go only by the words "fully waterproof" |
| Hood and field of view | Whether the hood fits over a helmet, whether the brim blocks your view, and whether it comes off, all directly affect your view while riding |
| Cuffs and trouser legs | Double cuffs or an elastic cuff make it harder for rain to run down the wrist into the sleeve, which matters especially if you wear gloves. Whether the trouser legs have a fastening at the hem affects how easily rain gets into your shoes |
| Reflective strips and size | A reflective strip on the back stands out more at night or in heavy rain. For size, look at height, chest, and waist, and leave room for your helmet and the clothes underneath. If you can, check the size chart before ordering |

## Choosing by Situation

- **Daily commuting, delivery, or driver work** — Look first at a two-piece with hard-wearing fabric, reflective strips, cuffs that keep water out, and a breathable design, so it is not too stuffy to wear for a long time.
- **Long rides in the rain, or windy** — Choose one with a removable hood or a brim, and with closing cuffs and trouser legs, to reduce rain getting in through the gaps.
- **You sit a lot and the seat wears things out** — The part of the rain pants that rubs on the seat wears through first. Look first at a type with thicker fabric in this area, or a shop that offers a hard-wearing version to add on.
- **You need it urgently** — Check how fast the shop says it ships. Some shops make you wait longer (for example 2 to 7 working days before it ships). If you need it in the rain, avoid those and choose one that says in stock and ships within 24 hours.

## More Important Than the Raincoat Itself: How to Wear and Store It So It Lasts

- The first time you wear it, try it on together with your helmet and check that the hood, sleeve length, and trouser length do not get in the way of riding.
- After a ride, try to spread it out to dry before storing it. If you fold it up wet and stuff it in the storage box, it can get a bad smell, and film material may stick together.
- Keep the reflective strip clean. Mud or oil on it lowers the reflection.
- If it still feels stuffy, first check whether you are wearing too many layers underneath, or whether the size is too small to leave any room for air.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which is better, a two-piece or a one-piece motorcycle raincoat?**
It depends on how you use it. People who commute every day, deliver food, work as drivers, or get on and off the bike often generally choose a two-piece, because you can put on, take off, and wash the top and bottom separately and you have more freedom to move while sitting on the bike. The downside is that you need to watch for water getting in where the jacket and pants overlap. A one-piece covers a larger area but is harder to put on and take off and is also hotter, so it suits people who only get occasional rain and need to be covered from head to toe.

**The raincoat feels stuffy to wear. Is there a fix?**
Waterproofing and breathability always involve a trade-off. When you buy, look for a mesh lining, a vented back, or vent holes. When you wear it, do not wear too many layers underneath, and choose a looser size so there is room for air to move, which also eases the stuffiness.

**Do rain pants wear through easily if you sit a lot?**
The part that rubs on the seat does tend to wear out first. When you choose, look at whether the rain pants have thicker fabric in this area, and some shops offer a hard-wearing version you can add on. Before you put them on, you can also put a layer on the seat to reduce direct rubbing.
