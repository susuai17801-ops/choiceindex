## Official Sources: What the Fire Safety Equipment Standard Says

- **Emergency lighting is part of the evacuation equipment** — Article 10 of the “Standards for Installation of Fire Safety Equipment in Various Places” (各類場所消防安全設備設置標準) divides evacuation equipment into three kinds: marking equipment, evacuation devices, and emergency lighting. The articles below come from the National Laws and Regulations Database, whose page shows a revision date of April 24, 2024 (we read the original text on November 13, 2026).
- **Which places must install it (Article 24)** — The article names rooms in places with certain uses, rooms in buildings with a total floor area of 1,000 square meters or more, and rooms whose effective daylight area is under 5 percent of the floor area, among others, as required to have emergency lighting. This is a rule for places, not guidance on which light a household should buy.
- **Battery capacity (Article 177)** — Emergency lighting must be connected to an emergency power supply, and that supply must use a battery with a capacity that keeps it running for at least 30 minutes.
- **Illuminance (Article 178)** — Illuminance of an emergency light at floor level must be at least 10 lux on the floor of underground passages in underground buildings, and at least 2 lux elsewhere; extra emergency lights must be added at corridor bends.
- **Places that may be exempt (Article 179)** — The article lists places that may be exempt, including rooms in collective housing (集合住宅, such as apartment buildings), restrooms, bathrooms, washrooms, storage rooms or machine rooms, and rooms on the evacuation floor within a 30-meter walking distance of an outdoor exit.
- **Whether an ordinary home must have one** — The articles we read do not say an ordinary home must install one. Whether other rules apply to rentals, guesthouses and the like, we did not check across all place categories; to be sure, ask your local fire department.

**Must an ordinary household install an emergency light?**
Articles 24 and 179 of the installation standard, which we read, are rules for particular places, and they do not say an ordinary home must install one. Whether other rules apply (for example to rentals or guesthouses), we did not check across the board; ask your local fire department.

**What is the difference between “國家認證” and “消防署認證”?**
We did not find an official explanation of these two store terms. The recommended product's specifications list a fire equipment type approval number, EM-A10008-6, which we did not check in the fire agency's system; if a store writes only words and no number, ask the seller.

**How many lumens should I choose?**
The installation standard we read sets only the illuminance of a place (2 lux and above, 10 lux for underground passages), not the lumens of a product; a master's thesis says the approval standard sets no requirement for brightness or power, but we did not read the approval standard itself. The recommended product's page does not give brightness, so ask the seller.
