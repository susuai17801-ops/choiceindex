Laundry detergent is something every household uses up every week, but the shelf has regular liquid, concentrated gel, anti-bacterial and anti-dust-mite types, indoor-drying types, perfume series, and both bottles and refill packs, and the claims on the package get louder and louder. ## First, Understand the 4 Common Types of Laundry Products

| Type | Features | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Regular liquid detergent | The most common basic type. You measure the amount as the package says, it dissolves quickly and spreads easily even in cold water. The price range is wide, and it comes as a bottle or a refill pack | Everyday clothes, people who want something simple and easy, and flexible budgets |
| Concentrated detergent (gel) | A higher share of active ingredients, so you usually use less per wash. The texture is thicker, and the package often highlights extras such as scent, softness, or anti-bacterial claims. How well it cleans depends on your clothes and water | People who want to use less, care about scent and softness, or have moderately dirty clothes |
| Laundry powder | Often compared on cleaning power and low cost, but it must dissolve fully in water, so watch for residue in cold water. Some types contain bleach or enzymes, so be careful with some fabrics | People with heavily soiled clothes, large loads, or who want to keep costs down |
| Laundry pods (capsules) | One fixed dose per pod, no measuring cup, and the film dissolves in water. But each pod costs more, and they are not suited to small loads, hand washing, or quick cycles. See our guide on laundry pods for a full comparison | People who do not want to measure, wash a similar load each time, and want convenience |

No single type is "best". It depends on how dirty your clothes are, how often you wash, your scent preference, and your budget. If you want a fixed dose and less hassle, consider pods. If you want to adjust the amount yourself and compare the cost per wash, liquid detergent or a refill pack is usually more flexible. Only heavily soiled clothes need a detergent chosen for strong cleaning power. Always read the product instructions, because the amount and the suitable fabrics differ by product. This page is only a buying guide, not product safety advice.

## 4 Things to Check When Choosing Laundry Detergent

| What to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Does the function match what you need? | Shops commonly group products as "indoor drying", "anti-bacterial and anti-dust-mite", "soft and color-protecting", or "for baby clothes". First think about what matters most to you. If you dry clothes indoors and worry about a damp smell, look for indoor-drying or odor-removing products. Only look for an anti-dust-mite claim if someone at home is sensitive to dust mites. These are mostly claims by the seller or brand, and the standards differ between brands, so the real effect varies with your home |
| Scent and where you use it | Detergent scents include floral, fruity, woody, soap, and unscented, and how strong and how lasting a scent is depends on the person. If someone at home is sensitive to smells, or you have babies or pets, start with a light scent or no scent, or buy a small size to try first, and do not stock up all at once |
| Volume, packaging, and cost per wash | Bottles and refill packs (pouches) differ a lot in volume and unit price, so compare "price ÷ number of washes" rather than only the total price. Refill packs are usually cheaper but you have to pour them into a bottle yourself, so be careful not to spill, and check whether the pouch has a zipper or can be resealed |
| Ingredient labels and certification sources | Some shops list enzymes, the type of surfactant, whether it is free of fluorescent agents or phosphorus, biodegradability, and tests such as SGS or certificates such as the European Ecocert. These are mostly the seller's claims, so before you order check that the package clearly shows the brand, ingredients, and manufacturer. We have not checked each of these claims |

## How to Work Out the Cost per Wash

To see whether a detergent is really good value, look at "how much does one wash cost", not only the price. It is simple: first find the "recommended amount" on the package or in the shop description (for example how many milliliters per wash, which depends on the water level and the load). Then work out the number of washes as "total volume ÷ recommended amount", and finally the cost per wash as "price ÷ number of washes".

- **For concentrated types, look at the dose, not the volume** — A concentrated detergent usually needs less per wash, so for the same volume it may give more washes than a regular one. But the recommended dose differs by product, so you have to calculate it to be sure.
- **Refill packs are usually cheaper than bottles, but you refill yourself** — A refill pack saves the cost of the bottle and packaging, so the unit price is usually lower. The downside is that you pour it into a bottle yourself, and you should check that the volume is what you want before you order.
- **Do not be led by "second item discount" or bundle prices** — A bundle can look cheap per unit, but if you are not sure about the scent and stock up more than you use, it is a waste. Check the shelf life as well.

## Safety Notes When Using Laundry Detergent

- **Use the amount on the package** — More detergent does not mean cleaner clothes. It can leave residue and need more rinsing. Too little may not clean well, so adjust to the water level and the load.
- **Do not mix with bleach or other cleaners** — Chlorine bleach mixed with an acidic cleaner or a cleaner of unknown type can release harmful gas. Use only one product at a time and keep the room ventilated.
- **If you have sensitive skin, try a small amount first** — Shops often write "suitable for sensitive skin" or "non-toxic", which are mostly the seller's claims and do not mean it will not irritate. If you have allergies, baby clothes, or sensitive skin, test with a small amount first and rinse once more.
- **Keep it out of reach of children and pets** — Store the detergent and refill packs away to avoid swallowing or contact. If it is swallowed or gets in the eyes, follow the instructions on the package and get medical help as soon as possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which is better: liquid detergent, laundry pods, or powder?**
There is no absolute best, it depends on how you use it. Liquid detergent has an adjustable dose and suits most households. Pods are a convenient fixed dose but cost more per pod. Powder suits heavier dirt or lower cost, but you must make sure it dissolves. Always follow the product instructions and the fabric of your clothes.

**Is concentrated detergent (gel) really cheaper to use?**
A concentrated type usually needs less per wash, so for the same volume it may give more washes. But the recommended dose differs by product, so you have to calculate "price ÷ number of washes" to be sure. Extras such as scent and softness also affect the price, so do not look only at the volume.

**What is the difference between a refill pack and a bottle, and which should I buy?**
A refill pack saves the bottle, so the unit price is usually lower, but you pour it into a bottle yourself. A bottle is ready to use. For your first purchase, buy a bottle or make sure you have an empty bottle at home, and then use refill packs to restock. Check the volume and option before you order.

**Can I trust labels like anti-bacterial, anti-dust-mite, and suitable for sensitive skin?**
These are mostly the seller's or brand's own claims, and the standards and test methods differ by brand. We have not checked each one. If someone at home has allergies or you have babies, go by what the package shows and try a small amount first. If it does not suit you, stop using it and get medical help.

**How long does the scent of laundry detergent last?**
How long the scent lasts depends on the water level, how you dry the clothes, the fabric, and your own sense of smell, and there is no common standard for the lasting time brands claim. It depends on personal use. If you worry about a scent that is too strong, start with a light scent or a small size.

**What should I watch for with detergent if I have children or pets?**
Keep detergent and refill packs out of reach of children and pets to avoid swallowing or contact. If it gets in the eyes, rinse with plenty of clean water and get medical help if it does not feel right. If it is swallowed, follow the instructions on the package and get medical help as soon as possible.
