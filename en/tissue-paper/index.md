Pull-out tissue is something almost every home, dormitory, and office stocks up on. But the material, the number of plies, whether it holds up when wet, and the number of sheets in a pack all differ. ## First, Tissue vs. Kitchen Paper Towels: What Is the Difference?

People often use the two as if they were the same thing, but they are not quite the same. Kitchen paper towels have coarser fibers and are designed to soak up water and oil more strongly, specifically for grease and liquids. Pull-out tissue has finer fibers and is meant to be gentler on skin. It is fine for wiping hands, wiping a table, and simple cleaning, but it is less durable than a kitchen paper towel when it has to soak up a lot of grease for a long time. Mixing them in daily use is fine; just do not expect pull-out tissue to fully replace the absorbency of a kitchen paper towel.

## 4 Things to Check When Choosing Pull-Out Tissue

| What to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Material | Virgin wood pulp is finer with less dust and is friendlier to sensitive skin. Recycled paper is more eco-friendly but usually feels rougher |
| Number of plies | More plies usually means thicker and more durable, but check whether the total number of pulls in the pack is lower as a result |
| Whether it holds up when wet | Thicker types labeled as wet-strength (可濕水) tear less easily and suit a kitchen. Ordinary types tear easily when wet |
| Cost per sheet or per gram | Pack sizes differ a lot. To compare accurately, compare total pulls × plies directly, or work out the unit price from total weight |

## Choosing by Situation

- **A large family, a company, or a shared dormitory** — Look first at a case of tissue that offers good value when bought in bulk, and compare the total pulls per case and the shipping threshold.
- **A kitchen with a lot of grease** — Choose a thicker type that says it absorbs well and does not tear when wet, and use it together with dedicated kitchen paper towels for different jobs.
- **You are sensitive to dust or the material** — Choose virgin wood pulp and a type that states it has no fluorescent whitening agents.
- **A small flat or limited dorm storage** — Choose a wall-mounted or box-type pull-out pack that hangs in one place and does not take up table space.

## More Important Than the Material: How to Work Out What Is a Good Deal

- How to work it out: total price ÷ (pulls per pack × plies) ≈ cost per "single-ply sheet", which lets you compare across brands.
- A case or group buy is usually cheaper than a single pack, but check the shipping threshold and how long delivery takes.
- When a pack says "more for the same price", check how the actual number of pulls changed, and do not go only by the enlarged number on the pack.
- Taiwan is humid, so if you stock up on a lot, watch how you store it so that it does not get damp and moldy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can pull-out tissue be used as kitchen paper towel?**
Ordinary pull-out tissue has finer fibers. It is fine for wiping hands, wiping a table, and simple cleaning. But when you have to soak up a lot of grease for a long time, or need something strong that does not tear, it is better to use it together with dedicated kitchen paper towels, because the absorbency and tear strength differ quite a lot.

**Is tissue with more plies always better?**
Not necessarily. The number of plies is only one measure of thickness, and you also need to look at the size of each sheet and the total number of pulls. With many plies but few pulls, the total amount you can use is not necessarily a better deal. To compare across brands, work out "total pulls × plies", or compare total weight directly.

**How should I store a case of pull-out tissue?**
Keep it somewhere dry and well ventilated, away from direct sunlight and damp (Taiwan is humid, and tissue that gets damp can go moldy or its fibers can soften). After opening, try to use it up within the shelf life. Keep the cases apart and do not stack them so high that the packs get crushed.
