# How to Choose an Electric Blanket in Taiwan: The Inspection Mark, Chinese Labels, Voltage and Wattage, and Washability (電熱毯)

Last updated: October 24, 2026
選品索引 ChoiceIndex — https://choiceindex.tw/en/

When winter comes and you search for an electric blanket on Shopee Taiwan, listings priced from a few hundred to over two thousand NT dollars are all marked "BSMI certified", and one model comes in single and double sizes, with some washable and some not, so it is easy to buy the wrong one. In Taiwan, an electric blanket is a product that must be inspected by the Bureau of Standards, Metrology and Inspection (BSMI). The tests cover temperature rise, leakage current and dielectric strength, abnormal operation, construction, and electromagnetic compatibility, and BSMI also checks the commodity inspection mark and the Chinese labels. This guide explains BSMI's buying and use tips, the market test results, how to read voltage and wattage, and a checklist to run before you order on Shopee.

## 5 Things to Check When Choosing an Electric Blanket

| What to check | What to look at | A common problem |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection mark and its number | BSMI advises checking that the product carries the commodity inspection mark. The mark is a symbol plus an identification number, and according to a BSMI briefing the identification number prefixes are R, D, T, Q, and M. Another BSMI document gives the lookup address for the mark as civil.bsmi.gov.tw/bsmi_pqn/. The specification section of a Shopee listing usually shows a BSMI number too, so check it before you order and save a photo of it | The title says "BSMI certified", but the specification section shows no number |
| Complete Chinese labeling | In BSMI's test checks, the label should include: the product name and model; the name or registered trademark of the maker or supplier; the rated voltage, frequency, and power consumption or rated current; the Class II symbol for Class II construction; and suitable warnings (for installation, use, or maintenance). BSMI's guide also advises checking that the responsible company's name and address are clear. Check it again when the blanket arrives | A nice package, but no labels on the blanket or in the manual |
| Voltage and wattage | Household voltage in Taiwan is 110 V. Most of the electric blankets in BSMI's test table are labeled 110 V 60 Hz, with labeled power ranging from 45 W to 170 W. If you bring a blanket from your home country, first check that its rated voltage matches Taiwan's 110 V. Wattage is just a labeled figure and a bigger number is not necessarily better, so follow the manual | Buying a product labeled 220 V, or looking only at wattage and not voltage |
| Washable or not washable | BSMI's guide says electric blankets are either washable or not washable, so read the manual before washing and decide how to wash it only after confirming whether the product is washable. If a listing says machine washable, still check how the manual says to wash and spin it | Putting a non-washable electric blanket in the washing machine |
| Size and placement | Measure the mattress or the place where it will go, then compare it with the single and double sizes on the listing, and with the length and width. Size descriptions differ between shops, so go by the listing page | Buying a double-size blanket for a single bed |

## What to Do After You Get It (From BSMI's Guide)

- **Lay it flat, do not fold it** — When in use, the electric blanket must be laid flat on the bed to prevent wrinkles, because bending or folding it too much can damage the heating wire, cause overheating, and lead to an accident.
- **Avoid direct contact with the skin** — BSMI advises putting a sheet or a thin blanket over the electric blanket.
- **Switch it off when you go out or are not using it** — BSMI's guide says to cut the power when you go out or are not using it, and not to let infants, people who are not alert, or people who do not sense heat well use it alone, to avoid burns.
- **Plug in firmly, and pull by the plug** — Before use, make sure the power plug fits tightly in the socket, and when you unplug it, hold the plug and do not pull the cord.
- **Read the manual before use** — Follow the manual and pay special attention to the warnings and notes. This page only collects BSMI's tips, so for actual use go by the product manual.

## Before You Order, Check These

- Measure the mattress or the place where it will go, and compare it with the listing's single and double sizes.
- The specification section should show a BSMI number. Take a screenshot of it.
- Look the identification number up in BSMI's commodity inspection mark lookup system. According to BSMI's briefing, for some M-series numbers you can only see the company name and no inspection record.
- Confirm that the rated voltage is 110 V, that the power consumption is labeled, and whether the listing says washable or not washable.
- When the blanket arrives, check the mark and the Chinese labels again, and read the manual before use.

## Quick Checklist Before You Buy

- Did you measure the size? → Measure the mattress or the place where it will go, and compare it with the single and double sizes
- Is there an inspection mark and a BSMI number? → Both the specification section and the blanket should show them, so take a screenshot or a photo and then look the number up in BSMI's system
- Are the Chinese labels complete? → Name and model, maker or supplier, rated voltage and power consumption, warnings, and also the company name and address

- Can it be washed? → Read the manual first, because washable and non-washable blankets are washed differently

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How did BSMI's market test turn out?**
According to BSMI's 2021 table of market test results for electric blankets, 10 samples were tested and 3 did not comply: one had a production date on its label that was not the real production date; one had a power plug with no holes in the pin blades, which did not meet CNS 690 and amounted to evading inspection; and one exceeded the limit in the electromagnetic compatibility (EMI) test. This is the result for one batch of samples on the market in one year and does not represent all products.

**How do I check the commodity inspection mark?**
A BSMI document gives the lookup address for the mark as civil.bsmi.gov.tw/bsmi_pqn/. According to a BSMI briefing, for some M-series numbers you can only see the company name and no inspection record. If the mark is unclear or you suspect the product skipped inspection, you can ask the supplier for the inspection certificate, and if neither is available, report it to BSMI. Follow the official system for the exact steps.

**Can I leave an electric blanket on all night?**
BSMI's guide gives no time rule for sleeping use. It only advises cutting the power when you go out or are not using it, and following the manual and its warnings. We do not give safety advice, so go by the product manual on whether it suits overnight use.

**Can I use it directly against my skin?**
BSMI's guide advises against direct contact with the skin and suggests putting a sheet or a thin blanket on top of it. It also advises against letting infants, people who are not alert, or people who do not sense heat well use it alone, to avoid burns.
