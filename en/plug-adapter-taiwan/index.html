How to Choose a Power Plug Adapter (轉接頭) for Taiwan: Round-Pin to Flat-Pin, and Check the Voltage First

Last updated: October 18, 2026

Household outlets in Taiwan typically supply 110V and use flat-pin plugs. In many other countries, including Indonesia, household outlets supply around 220–230V, and plugs are often round-pin. If you bring a charger, fan, or appliance from home to Taiwan, the first thing you may notice is that the plug does not fit. Shopee has many adapters, but most of them are travel adapters made for people from Taiwan going abroad, which is the opposite direction. More important: an adapter only changes the shape of the plug. It does not change the voltage. This guide explains the types of adapters, 5 things to check before you buy, and why you must read the label on your device first.

There is no single "best" adapter. Check the voltage label on your device first, then the plug shape and the power it uses. A phone or laptop charger with an input of 100–240V works with a Euro-to-US adapter. Devices that say only 220V, such as rice cookers, electric kettles, and fans, should not be forced to work in Taiwan. It is better to buy a device sold in Taiwan (110V), or ask your employer, landlord, or a qualified electrician. This page is only a buying guide, not electrician advice or a safety standard. Safety When Using a Plug Adapter

  • An adapter does not change the voltage — It only changes the shape of the plug. Taiwan uses 110V, while many devices from home use around 220V. If a device's input voltage range does not include 110V, do not use it just because the plug fits.
  • Read the label before you buy — The "INPUT" line on a device or charger shows the voltage range it accepts. The input voltage range must include 110V (for example, a label that says 100–240V) for the device to work properly on a Taiwan outlet.
  • Do not connect high-power heating appliances — Electric kettles, rice cookers, irons, hair dryers, and heaters use a lot of power, and a small adapter can easily overheat. Use appliances made for Taiwan's electricity.
  • Do not stack or chain too many — Avoid stacking several adapters or extension cords. The outlet and the cables may carry more current than they can handle.
  • Replace it if it is too loose — If the plug and adapter do not fit tightly, wobble, or feel loose, they can overheat and even spark. Use a size that fits.
  • Stop if it is hot, smells burnt, or changes color — If it feels hot to the touch, smells burnt, or the casing changes color or shape while in use, unplug it right away and do not use it again.
  • Unplug when not in use, and keep it away from damp places — Unplug it when you are not using it, do not use it in damp places such as a bathroom or next to a sink, and keep it out of reach of children.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an adapter change 220V to 110V?
No. An adapter only changes the shape of the plug, not the voltage. If the device label says 100–240V, an adapter is enough. If it says only 220V or 230V, do not force it to work on a 110V outlet in Taiwan.
Can my phone charger use an adapter directly?
Most phone and laptop chargers have an input label of 100–240V, so if the plug shape is different, an "into Taiwan" adapter is enough. Still check the INPUT label on your own charger to be sure.
Can I use an adapter with a rice cooker or an electric kettle?
These devices use a lot of power. If they say only 220V, an adapter cannot solve it, and the adapter may overheat because of the high current. It is better to use a device sold in Taiwan (110V).
Do flat-pin plugs from Vietnam or the Philippines need an adapter?
Some flat-pin plugs are similar in shape to Taiwan outlets and may fit directly. But voltage and pin shape differ by country and by product, so still check the voltage on the label, and if the fit is not right or is loose, do not force it.
One shop page lists several adapter directions. How do I choose?
Choose the "into Taiwan" option, which converts a European round-pin plug to a US (Taiwan) flat-pin plug. Read the option name once more before you order so you do not pick the opposite direction, from Taiwan to abroad.
What should I do if the adapter gets hot?
Unplug it right away and stop using it. Possible reasons include a device that uses too much power, a loose plug with poor contact, or poor adapter quality. Do not keep using it, and do not stack other adapters or extension cords.
