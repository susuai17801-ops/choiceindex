# How to Choose Static-Cling Frosted Window Film (靜電窗貼) in Taiwan: Width, Length, Privacy Level, and Renting Tips

Last updated: October 24, 2026

A rented room or dorm window that faces a corridor, or a bathroom window that neighbors can see into, makes you want some privacy, but you worry about the hassle of removing it when you move out. That is why static-cling frosted film is a common choice. But shops sell pure frosted, white frosted, gray frosted, and fully opaque black, in widths from 30 to 150 cm, and the price steps up with the width. Pick the wrong width or material, and either people can still see in or the room gets too dark. This guide explains the 3 types of window film, 5 things to check, and how to measure your glass and work out the length to buy, so you can check once before you order.

## First, Understand the 3 Common Types of Window Film

| Type | Features | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Static-cling frosted film (no glue) | It sticks to the glass with static and a layer of water, with no glue. Usually it comes off without leaving residue, and if you put it on crooked you can lift it and place it again. The surface is frosted or matte, so light comes through but you cannot see clearly in or out | It only suits smooth glass, and textured or rough glass holds it badly. In damp places the edges may lift, and there is no guarantee how many times you can reuse it after taking it off |
| Adhesive-backed frosted film | It has glue on the back, so it sticks more firmly and the edges are less likely to curl. It gives the same frosted cover | When you remove it, it may leave residue or mark the glass surface, so a renter should check with the landlord first. It is hard to reposition if you put it on crooked |
| Heat-insulating / tinted film | It stresses blocking light or heat, is darker in color, usually lets less light through than ordinary frosted film, and often also gives privacy | The room gets darker, and whether it suits special glass (double-pane, tempered, or coated glass) should be checked with the seller or the building manager, not decided on your own |

No single type is "the best". It mainly depends on how much cover you need, whether the glass is smooth, and whether you are renting and have to restore the window when you leave. If you want to block the view but be able to remove it later, static-cling film with no glue is usually the first choice, and only consider adhesive film if you want it to stick firmly and do not mind removal. Also remember that frosted film makes things blurry, not invisible: in the daytime, when it is bright outside, it is harder to see inside, but at night with the lights on indoors, people outside may still see silhouettes. If you need full cover, choose an opaque type or add a curtain. This page only explains how to choose and is not product safety advice.

## What to Remember When Installing and Using It

- **Clean the glass before you apply it** — Dust, oil film, and limescale on the glass stop the film from sticking well and cause bubbles. The corners collect dust, so clean them especially well.
- **Spray plenty of water, then squeegee it flat** — The usual steps are to spray plenty of clean water on the glass, peel off the backing, place the film and adjust its position, and then squeegee from the middle outward to push out the water and bubbles. Press it flat along the frame, trim the extra, and finally wipe the edges dry.
- **Be careful with the blade when trimming** — When you trim with a knife, keep the blade flat and close to the frame, take care not to scratch the window frame, and keep children away from the blade.
- **If you rent, check first whether you may apply it** — Even with static film that uses no glue, we suggest asking the landlord or the manager first whether window film is allowed, and keeping the instructions from the pack so you can remove it before you move out.
- **After removal you may not be able to reapply it** — There is no guarantee how many times static film can be reused. After taking it off, store it flat or rolled to avoid creases and dust, and spray water again when you reapply it.

## If You Rent, Live in a Dorm, or Buy for Your Employer's Home, Check These First

- Measure the width and height of each piece of glass you want to cover (left to right is the width, top to bottom is the height), and note how many pieces there are.
- Check whether the glass is smooth, or textured or frosted, and if you are not sure, ask the landlord, the manager, or the seller.
- Decide how much cover you want: pure frosted that only blurs the view, or white, gray, or black frosted that blocks it.
- Ask first whether the landlord agrees to window film, and whether you must restore the glass when you leave.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will static-cling window film fall off on its own?**
It can. Static film sticks with water and static, and only suits smooth glass. If the glass has dust, limescale, or texture, or it is in a damp place or one with big temperature swings, the edges may lift. Cleaning the glass well before you apply it and squeegeeing out all the water and bubbles usually makes it less likely to fall. If an edge lifts badly, you can spray water and apply it again.

**With frosted window film on, can people outside still see in at night?**
They can. Frosted film blurs the view, but it does not block it completely: at night, with the lights on inside and darker outside, people outside may still see silhouettes. For more privacy, choose white, gray, or opaque film, or add a curtain.

**How do width and price work?**
The same product is often priced in steps by width and calculated per cm of length. First measure the glass width, choose a size no smaller than the glass, and then multiply the length you need by the price per cm to estimate the total. Go by the listing page for prices and sizes, and because different sellers calculate price differently, convert to one method when you compare.

**I rent. What should I do with static film when I move out?**
Static film with no glue can usually be peeled off without leaving residue, but we still suggest asking the landlord first. Before you peel, lift a corner slowly and spray water to help it come off. If there is limescale or a mark left on the glass, wipe it with clean water or glass cleaner. It depends on the product and the glass, so go by the listing and the pack instructions.
