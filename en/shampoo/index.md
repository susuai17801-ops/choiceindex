# How to Choose Shampoo (洗髮精) in Taiwan: Silicone-Free, Fragrance, and the Real Cost per ml

Last updated: October 23, 2026
選品索引 ChoiceIndex — https://choiceindex.tw/en/

Shampoos all wash your hair, but a basic cleansing shampoo, a perfume and plant-extract shampoo, and a treatment shampoo (anti-dandruff, oil control, repair for dyed or permed hair) make very different claims, and the price can differ by several times. This guide explains the 3 types, 4 things to check, and how to work out what is really a good deal, so you can decide which one to buy.

## First, Understand the 3 Types of Shampoo

| Type | Features | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Basic cleansing | Focuses on basic cleaning power with fairly simple ingredients, and usually has the lowest unit price | People who put price first, have an oily scalp, or wash their hair every day |
| Perfume / fragrance and plant extract | Stresses a layered fragrance that lasts, plus moisturizing ingredients such as hyaluronic acid or plant extracts, and often comes with a "silicone-free" claim. The unit price is medium to high | People who care about a lasting scent after washing and want moisture with a clean feel |
| Treatment (anti-dandruff / oil control / color care / damage repair) | Has extra ingredients aimed at a specific problem such as dandruff, hair loss, or damage from dyeing and perming. The price is usually the highest | People with a specific scalp or hair problem (flakes, itching, breakage, faded color) |

No single type is "the best". It depends on how oily your scalp is, whether you have a specific problem, and how much extra you are willing to pay for fragrance or treatment. Words such as "plant extract" and "natural" are marketing terms, not a guarantee of how much of an ingredient there is or how well it works, so you still need to read the ingredient list.

## 4 Things to Check When Choosing Shampoo

| What to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Silicone and how it feels | Silicone coats the hair, so it feels smooth and easy to comb right after washing, but if you do not rinse well over a long time it can feel built up. Silicone-free shampoo feels lighter and is gentler on the scalp, but may feel a little dry when you first switch, and you can use a conditioner to make up the smoothness. Which is better depends on your hair and habits, and there is no absolute answer |
| pH and scalp sensitivity | A mildly acidic shampoo (for example pH 5.5) is closer to the natural pH of the scalp. Look at this first if your scalp is sensitive or your hair is often dyed or permed. A more alkaline cleanser may leave the scalp drier and tighter |
| How long the fragrance lasts | An ordinary fragrance fades soon after the hair dries. Types that state a technology such as "microcapsule slow release" last longer, but they usually have more fragrance ingredients too. If you are sensitive to fragrance, pregnant, or breastfeeding, try a small amount first |
| Certification and ingredient labels | Specific labels, such as a third-party organic certification (for example Ecocert) or "no parabens", are more useful than the words "plant extract" or "natural" alone |

## How to Work Out the Real Cost per ml

Do not look only at "how much for one bottle". Work out "total price ÷ volume (ml)" to get the price per ml, so you can fairly compare different sizes and types. For example, if 700ml costs $300, that is about $0.43 per ml. If 350ml costs $180, that is about $0.51 per ml, so the larger bottle usually has the lower unit price. But watch the shelf life after opening and how often you actually use it, because a bottle that is too big to finish before it expires is a waste. For a product where you can pick any of several scents, the same size usually has the same price, so you can choose by scent alone without affecting how good a deal it is.

## Choosing by Situation

- **You wash your hair every day and your scalp is oily** — Look first at a light formula, and avoid treatment types that stress heavy moisture. Basic cleansing or silicone-free types usually feel lighter.
- **Dyed or permed hair that is damaged or splits and breaks easily** — Choose a treatment type that lists specific repair ingredients such as amino acids, and do not buy it just because the pack says "repair".
- **A sensitive scalp that easily turns red or itchy** — Look first at a mildly acidic formula with as simple an ingredient list as possible, and try a small amount first to see how your scalp reacts.
- **You care about a lasting scent, or want something to give as a gift or use yourself** — A perfume / fragrance and plant-extract type is the main choice. You can pick by scent, and a series with several scents to choose from is good for using in turns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is silicone-free shampoo really better?**
Silicone-free is not automatically better; it is a trade-off. Silicone coats the hair, so it feels smooth and easy to comb right after washing, but if you do not rinse well over a long time it can feel built up on the hair or scalp. Silicone-free shampoo feels lighter and is gentler on the scalp, but may feel a little dry when you first switch, and if you need to you can use a conditioner to make up the smoothness. Which suits you depends on your hair, how oily your scalp is, and your habits, and silicone-free is not necessarily better.

**How is the lasting scent of a perfume shampoo different from an ordinary fragrance?**
An ordinary fragrance mostly fades soon after the hair dries. In a perfume shampoo that states "top, middle, and base notes" or a "microcapsule slow release" technology, the scent molecules are held in tiny capsules and released gradually as the hair rubs and as your body warms, so the scent lasts longer. But a scented shampoo usually has more fragrance ingredients than an unscented one, so if you are sensitive to fragrance, pregnant, or breastfeeding, check the ingredients on the pack first, or test a small area of skin before using it all over.
