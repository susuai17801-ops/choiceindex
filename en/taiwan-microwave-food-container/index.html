Microwave-Safe Food Containers in Taiwan: How to Read PP Material and Heat-Resistance Labels (保鮮盒)

Last updated: November 23, 2026

When you buy a food storage container, the first questions are: can it go in the microwave, what is it made of, and what temperature can it take? In Taiwan, plastic food containers must carry their material and heat-resistance temperature before sale, but many people are unsure where the label is, how to read it, and whether "heat resistant" means "microwave-safe". This page covers the official labeling rules, what to check before microwaving, what to look for when buying, and one container that the seller lists as polypropylene (PP) and microwavable.

Taiwan's Rules: What Plastic Food Containers Must Be Labeled With

According to issue 1052 (November 14, 2025) of the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA) weekly safety bulletin, food containers must be labeled before sale with the product name, material name, heat-resistance temperature, usage notes, and whether they are reusable or single-use. Another TFDA article says containers or packaging made from July 2017 whose food-contact surface contains plastic must show the material name and suitable temperature on the smallest sales package or on the item itself. The underlying rule is the Sanitation Standard for Food Utensils, Containers and Packaging (amended January 11, 2023); plastic materials must meet the general rules plus Annex 2. We did not read the limits in Annex 2, so we give no figures here.

How to Read the Label

The container body, base, or packaging carries several items. Here is our summary:

Label itemWhat it meansHow to read it
Product nameThe name of the container, for example food storage containerConfirms it is a food container
Material nameFor example polypropylene (PP), polyethylene (PE), siliconeThe body and lid may be different materials, so check both
Heat-resistance temperatureThe temperature the container can withstand, for example −20℃ to 140℃This is the container's heat range; heat-resistant does not automatically mean microwave-safe, so also look for a microwave mark and instructions
Reusable / single-useWhether it can be washed and used againIf marked single-use, do not reuse it for food
Usage notesFor example open the vent when microwaving, not for very oily foodFollow them; the TFDA also advises avoiding long contact with hot, oily, or acidic food

A 2017 TFDA article advises using plastic containers within the labeled heat-resistance temperature; PP and PC are common plastics with higher heat resistance, but even heat-resistant materials should avoid long contact with hot, acidic, or oily food.

Before It Goes in the Microwave, Check These

  • Is it marked microwavable? — Heat resistance and microwave use are two different things. Whether the product or packaging says it is microwavable, and whether you must open the lid or vent, is what counts. We did not find an official document stating that "PP means microwave-safe".
  • Check the lid too — The body and lid can be different materials. The seller of our pick lists the body and lid as polypropylene and the lid frame as thermoplastic elastomer, all rated −20℃ to 140℃, but its instructions require the vent to be open when microwaving.
  • The kind of food — The TFDA advises avoiding long contact with hot, very oily, or acidic food. The seller of our pick also writes that it is not suitable for very oily food.
  • A more cautious official reminder — A 2011 press release from the TFDA's predecessor agency advised putting food for the microwave in ceramic or glass and removing plastic wrap. It is an older, more cautious reminder; if unsure, a ceramic or glass plate is the simplest choice.
  • Scratched, cloudy, or degraded: replace — The 2017 TFDA article says plastic items that are worn, cloudy, or degraded should be replaced right away; the 2011 press release also says to replace scratched ones promptly.

What to Look for When Buying

  • Material and heat resistance clearly stated — Both the product page and the item itself show the material name and heat-resistance temperature. A listing that only says "PP" and "microwavable" without a temperature is incomplete.
  • Specific usage limits — For example whether the vent must be open, whether oily food is allowed, whether it is dishwasher-safe. The more specific, the easier to check.
  • Single-use or reusable — Thin takeaway boxes are usually designed for single use; the TFDA article says reusing single-use items raises hygiene risk, so check the label.
  • Origin and seller information — Whether the listing shows the country of manufacture and the distributor. Our pick states made in Vietnam and an official Taiwan distributor; we did not verify this.

Is a container marked PP (number 5) always microwave-safe?
Not necessarily. A TFDA article lists PP as a more heat-resistant material, but whether it can go in the microwave depends on the labeled heat-resistance temperature and instructions.
