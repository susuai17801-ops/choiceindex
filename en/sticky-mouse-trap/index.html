How to Choose a Sticky Mouse Trap (黏鼠板) in Taiwan: Glue Boards, Live Traps, Electric Traps, and Poison Compared

Last updated: October 24, 2026

When you hear noises in the wall of your home or rental, or find mouse droppings, the first reaction is usually to buy some kind of trap online to hold things off. But glue boards, live-catch cages, electric traps, and rodent poison are handled very differently: some are troublesome to deal with, and some are risky for pets and children. This guide compares them side by side, so you can first work out which one suits you.

First, Understand the 4 Common Ways to Catch Mice

MethodHow well it worksSafetyHow hard it is to deal with
Glue board (sticky trap)Works well on young mice and small mice, but a bigger mouse may break freeNo poison and no electricity, so it is relatively safe for people and petsWhat you catch is alive, so handling it takes some mental preparation
Live-catch cageSteady results, and it can be reusedLeaves no poison residue, so it is the safestYou have to release the mouse yourself, which takes more time
Electric trapKills quicklyIt needs power, so watch for electric shock risk if there are young children or petsCleaning up is simple, but be careful where you place it
Rodent poison (bait)Strong, and works fast over a large areaPets can be poisoned if they eat it, so it is not recommended for homes with petsA poisoned mouse may die in a corner, wall, or ceiling you cannot see, and later there may be a smell

There is no "best" method, only the one that best suits where you live. Whether you have cats, dogs, or children, and whether you live in a rental, directly decides which ones to avoid.

Choosing by Situation

  • A rental room or dorm (common for migrant workers and students) — Look first at glue boards. They need no power, you do not need to keep a tool like a live-catch cage for the long term, and when you move out you can simply throw them away.
  • Cats, dogs, or young children at home — Avoid rodent poison because of the risk of swallowing it. Consider a live-catch cage first, or put glue boards in gaps and behind furniture that pets and children cannot reach at all.
  • A rooftop add-on, warehouse, or factory (a large area) — Use electric traps, or place many glue boards with bait. In this kind of space, covering a wide area matters more than how strong a single trap is.
  • A serious infestation you want to fix fast — Poison works faster, but be ready to deal with a smell later from a body you cannot find, and it is not recommended where people and pets move around a lot.

More Important Than the Tool: Where You Put It

Even a strong glue board loses half its effect if it is in the wrong place. The basic rules:

  • Put them along the walls — Mice like to move along walls and avoid open space, so put glue boards along wall corners, behind furniture, and where pipes run. They are usually much less effective in the middle of a room.
  • Add a little bait — A little peanut butter or a nut in the middle of the board clearly makes it more attractive.
  • Keep them dry — In damp places the glue loses stickiness, so do not put them where rain or damp can reach, and replace a damp board.
  • Use several at once — Placing several boards in different corners at the same time works better than one board, especially in a larger space.

Quick Checklist Before You Buy

  • A rental, and you do not want to keep tools long term? → Choose glue boards, which need no power and can be thrown away when you move out
  • Cats, dogs, or young children at home? → Avoid rodent poison, and use a live-catch cage or put boards where pets and children cannot reach
  • A large space (warehouse, rooftop add-on, factory)? → Electric traps, or many glue boards, with coverage first
  • A serious infestation you want to fix fast? → Poison works fast, but be ready for a smell from a body in an unknown place
  • Bought glue boards but catch nothing? → Check that they are on a path along the wall and that the board is not damp

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the best place to put a glue board?
Mice like to move along walls and avoid open floors, so put glue boards along wall corners, behind furniture, and where pipes run. They are usually much less effective in the middle of a room. You can put a little peanut butter or a nut in the middle of the board to make it more attractive.
What should I do after a glue board catches a mouse?
We suggest wearing gloves the whole time, folding the whole board with the mouse in it in half and sealing it, or putting it in a plastic bag and tying it tightly, and throwing it away with ordinary trash, so you never touch it with bare hands. If you are afraid of handling a live mouse, consider a live-catch cage first, or ask family or cleaning staff to help.
The glue board keeps catching nothing. Does that mean it does not work?
Not necessarily. Common reasons are that it is in the wrong place (not on a path along the wall), the board is damp so the glue is weaker, or the mouse is big and pulled free. Try other corners, put several boards at the same time to cover more area, and make sure the board is not damp or covered in dust. If the infestation is serious, you may need a live-catch cage as well, or a professional pest control service.
