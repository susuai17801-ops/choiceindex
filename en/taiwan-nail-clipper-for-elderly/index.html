Nail Clippers for Elderly People in Taiwan: Straight or Curved Blade, Thick Toenails, and No-Scatter Designs (指甲剪)

Last updated: November 28, 2026

As people age, their fingernails and toenails often become thicker and harder, and bending down to cut their own toenails gets difficult, so family members or caregivers often do it for them. Nail clippers (指甲剪) look much the same, but whether the blade is straight or curved, whether the jaw opens wide enough, and whether there is a catcher for the clippings make a big difference. This page sums up how to choose, how Taiwan hospital guides say to cut toenails, and then recommends one regular clipper and one wide-jaw clipper for thick nails.

Hospital guides say toenails should be cut straight across, so when cutting an elderly person's toenails, a straight-blade clipper is easier to control than a curved one. For fingernails, either type works, depending on what the person is used to.

How to Choose

  • Separate clippers for hands and feet — Use a curved S or M size for fingernails and a straight L size for toenails; keeping two separate clippers is also more hygienic.
  • Very thick toenails — If the nail will not fit in a regular clipper, choose one labeled wide jaw (大開口) or for thick nails (厚甲); do not force it.
  • No-scatter design — A clear cover or catcher keeps clippings from flying across the floor, which makes cleanup easier when you cut an elderly person's nails beside the bed.
  • Easy to squeeze — If the caregiver is doing the squeezing, or the elderly person cuts their own nails, choose a larger clipper that is easy to grip; one with a file lets you smooth the corners.
  • One clipper per person — If someone at home has nail fungus, it is best to keep their clippers separate (this is our own reminder).

Key Points for Cutting an Elderly Person's Toenails

The points below are our rewrite of the guide “Diabetic Foot Care (糖尿病足部照護)” from Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital (based on material from Taiwan's Health Promotion Administration) and a guide from the nursing department of the Kaohsiung Armed Forces General Hospital Zuoying Branch. For details, follow the guidance of a doctor or nurse:

  • 1. Good lighting — If the elderly person cannot see well or cannot reach their feet, have a family member cut their nails for them (Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide).
  • 2. Cut straight across — Cut toenails straight and flat, not into a curve or a point (Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide).
  • 3. Not too short — Do not cut toenails shorter than the tip of the toe; try to trim only the part that extends past the nail bed (Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital and Zuoying hospital guides).
  • 4. Leave the corners — Leave both corners of the nail extending beyond the nail groove and do not cut them off, so the nail does not grow into the skin (ingrown toenail) and cause an infection around the nail (Zuoying hospital guide).
  • 5. File afterwards — After cutting, smooth the edges with a nail file (Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide).

When to Ask a Doctor or Nurse First

  • Elderly people with diabetes: the Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide explains that people with diabetes often have blood vessel and nerve damage, so their feet lose feeling, get infected easily, and heal slowly; take extra care when cutting their toenails.
  • Do not cut corns or calluses yourself or use corn plasters; have them treated at a medical facility (Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide).
  • If the skin beside a nail is red, swollen, painful, or has pus, or the nail is already growing into the skin, have a doctor look at it first; do not dig at it or cut it yourself (this is our own reminder).
  • Nails that become thick, discolored, or misshapen may be affected by nail fungus or another condition; a wide-jaw clipper only makes them easier to cut and does not treat the underlying condition, so ask a doctor first (this is our own reminder).
  • If the elderly person takes blood thinners or bleeds easily, cut more slowly and avoid nicking the skin (this is our own reminder).

Frequently Asked Questions

In Taiwan, should an elderly person's toenails be cut round or straight?
Straight. The Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide says toenails should be cut straight across, not into a curve or point, and then filed smooth; the Zuoying hospital guide also reminds you not to cut off the corners so the nail does not grow into the skin.
What if the toenails are too thick to fit in a regular nail clipper?
Choose a clipper labeled wide jaw or for thick nails, or the KAI size L clipper with a straight blade, which KAI's official site says suits hard toenails. If the nails are thick and also discolored or misshapen, it may be nail fungus, so ask a doctor first.
Can an elderly person with diabetes cut their own toenails?
The Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital guide says people with diabetes have less feeling in their feet and heal slowly; if they cannot see well or cannot reach their feet, a family member should help. Cut straight across, not too short, and see a doctor if there is a wound or swelling.
What does “no-scatter” (防噴濺) mean on a nail clipper?
The clipper has a cover or catcher underneath or inside that collects the clippings so they do not fly onto the floor. KAI's official site says type001 and W001 both have this cover; the wide-jaw clipper's seller describes a latched catcher.
