## Buying for an Older Adult or Patient: Whom to Ask First

- **Ask the nurse or long-term care case manager first** — We do not judge which absorbency suits whom, and we do not discuss skin or care methods. For which type to use and how often to change it, ask a nurse, a home care nurse, or a long-term care case manager (照管專員).
- **What we found in official sources** — The Food and Drug Administration statement “Clarification on assistive devices being managed as medical devices” (created October 31, 2018; updated May 23, 2024) says most assistive products today are ordinary goods, and only those that meet the medical device definition under the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act are managed as medical devices; its examples are walking aids and the like, and it does not mention care underpads. So we cannot say whether care underpads are medical devices, and we found no official buying guide or absorbency standard.
- **Confirm where it will be used** — A bed, a wheelchair, and a sofa need different sizes; measure before buying. You can also note what is used now and what falls short, and ask the seller.
