## Official Sources: Taiwan's Night Lighting Rule and the Fine

- **Lights must be on at night** — Article 128 of Taiwan's Road Traffic Safety Rules (道路交通安全規則) says slow vehicles (慢車) traveling at night must turn on their lights. Under Article 69 of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act (道路交通管理處罰條例), slow vehicles include pedal bicycles, power-assisted bicycles, and micro electric two-wheelers (National Laws and Regulations Database, read November 14, 2026).
- **The fine for riding at night without lights** — Article 73, Paragraph 1, Subparagraph 5 of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act says a slow vehicle rider who does not turn on lights when riding at night is fined NT$300 to NT$1,200. The law's page notes that the amended version of this article took effect on October 31, 2026.
- **Lights must be kept in good condition** — Article 119 of the Road Traffic Safety Rules says slow vehicles must keep brakes, bells, lights, reflectors, and other safety equipment in good and complete condition. Article 72 of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act says that if lights and reflectors are not kept in good and complete condition as required, the owner of the slow vehicle is fined NT$300 to NT$1,200 and ordered to install or fix them within a deadline.
- **Power-assisted bicycles have their own rules** — Article 119 also says the safety equipment of power-assisted bicycles and micro electric two-wheelers must meet the safety testing standards, and their electronic control devices or original specifications may not be added to, removed, or changed without authorization. We did not check whether adding lights counts as a change; ask a bike shop first.
- **Light colors and inspection** — We did not find a written rule on the color, brightness, or mounting position of lights on pedal bicycles, and we did not find whether bicycle lights are products subject to mandatory inspection by the Bureau of Standards, Metrology and Inspection (標準檢驗局). The recommended product's page does not show a BSMI mark.
