# How to Wash a Bedridden Elder's Hair in Bed in Taiwan: Inflatable or Rigid Hair Washing Basin (洗頭槽)

If the elder you care for has been bedridden for a long time and cannot get to the bathroom, you can wash their hair in bed using a hair washing basin (洗頭槽 / 洗頭盆). On Shopee Taiwan there are two main types: inflatable (soft) basins that you inflate, and rigid (plastic) basins that need no inflating. This page sums up the differences and how to choose, the steps for washing hair in bed from a Taiwan hospital's home-care guide, and then recommends one product of each type.

## Inflatable vs. Rigid: What Is the Difference

The hospital home-care guide only says “hair washing basin” and does not say which type is better; in caregiver discussions, both types have their supporters. The table below is our own summary from product listings and caregiver discussions, not a test:

|  | Inflatable (soft) | Rigid (plastic) |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Like a round or square inflatable ring that takes shape once inflated | A plastic basin with a fixed shape |
| Neck | Softer; the amount of air can be adjusted | Harder; you can put a towel under the neck |
| Storage | Folds up when deflated and takes little space | Fixed shape, so it needs storage space |
| Before use | Must be pumped or blown up; it can sag if under-inflated | Ready to use right away |
| Watch for | Air leaks; dry it fully before putting it away | Whether its size fits the space beside the bed |

If you do not have a basin, the Penghu Hospital guide also says you can roll up both sides of a rubber draw sheet or waterproof plastic sheet and place it under the person's head instead.

## How to Choose

- **Small space, occasional use** — An inflatable basin is easy to store once deflated, making it suitable for caregivers living in their employer's home or in a small dorm.
- **Worried about it sagging** — A rigid basin needs no air and keeps its shape; because it is harder under the neck, you can add a towel.
- **Drainage** — Check for a drain hose or drain opening so water can flow into a bucket beside the bed; if the listing does not say, ask the seller.
- **Size** — Measure the width of the bed and the space for the bucket; rigid basins are usually longer (our pick is about 75 cm), so you need space beside the bed.
- **Pump or no pump** — Some inflatable basins come without a pump, so you blow them up yourself or buy a pump separately.

## Steps for Washing Hair in Bed

The steps below are our rewrite of the home-care guide “Washing Hair in Bed (床上洗頭)” from Penghu Hospital, Ministry of Health and Welfare, Taiwan. For details, follow the guidance of a doctor or nurse:

- **1. Prepare** — A hair washing basin, a bucket for waste water, a bucket of clean warm water, shampoo, towels, and a hair dryer. You can lay a waterproof pad on the bed first.
- **2. Position the basin** — Place the basin under the elder's head, with the drain leading to the waste water bucket.
- **3. Test the water** — Test the warm water with your wrist or elbow; if the elder is fully alert, ask whether the temperature feels right.
- **4. Wet and wash** — Wet the hair first, put shampoo in your palm, and apply it to the hair, then rub the scalp and hair with your fingertips (not your nails).
- **5. Rinse** — Rinse until all the foam is gone.
- **6. Wrap and remove the basin** — Wrap the hair in a towel, then take the basin away.
- **7. Dry** — Towel-dry the hair, use a hair dryer to finish drying it, and then comb it neatly.

## Points to Note

- The elder usually lies flat for hair washing; if they have difficulty breathing, use a semi-reclining position (Penghu Hospital guide).
- Set the water temperature by how it feels to the elder, not too hot or too cold, generally about 40–43℃ (Penghu Hospital guide).
- Use your fingertips and do not scratch with your nails, to avoid hurting the scalp; change the sheets if they get wet (Penghu Hospital guide).
- People with a head injury or recent head surgery should not have their hair washed for the time being (Penghu Hospital guide).
- If the elder feels unwell, has a fever, or has tubes in place (such as a nasogastric tube or tracheostomy tube), ask a doctor or nurse first whether and how to wash their hair (this is our own reminder).
- In cold weather, close the windows and get everything ready before you start, and dry the hair as soon as possible afterwards (this is our own reminder).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**In Taiwan, can you wash an elder's hair in bed without a hair washing basin?**
Yes. The Penghu Hospital home-care guide says you can roll up both sides of a rubber draw sheet or waterproof plastic sheet and place it under the person's head instead of a basin, with a waste water bucket below.

**What water temperature should I use to wash someone's hair in bed?**
The Penghu Hospital guide says to go by how it feels to the elder, generally about 40–43℃, and you can test it first with your wrist or elbow. If the elder is fully alert, ask them directly whether it is too hot.

**When should you not wash an elder's hair in bed?**
The Penghu Hospital guide says people with a head injury or recent head surgery should not have their hair washed for the time being. If the elder has a fever, feels unwell, or has tubes in place, ask a doctor or nurse first.

**How much air should an inflatable hair washing basin have?**
The seller of our inflatable pick says to inflate it to about 80% full, then adjust to suit the elder. With too little air, the basin can sag and water may spill over.
