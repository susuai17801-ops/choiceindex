How to Choose Dish Soap (洗碗精) in Taiwan: Ingredients, Certifications, Concentration, and Whether Refill Packs Save Money

Last updated: October 23, 2026

You use dish soap every day to wash bowls and pots, but on the shelf an ordinary synthetic soap, a plant-based soap, and a soap with an international certification can differ in price by several times. This guide explains the types by ingredient, the certification labels, concentration, and packaging, and shows you how to work out whether a refill pack is really worth it, so you can see what actually matters before you decide which one to buy.

First, Understand the 3 Types of Dish Soap

TypeFeaturesWho it is for
Ordinary synthetic surfactantsUsually cleans better and costs less. Some ingredients (such as LAS) are more irritating to skin, and people more often raise concerns about residue after long contactPeople who put price first, do not soak their hands for long when washing up, or have many greasy pots
Natural / plant-basedMade with surfactants from plant sources such as coconut oil and sugar cane, and marketed as biodegradable. Usually gentler on skin, but may clean less strongly and cost morePeople who wash by hand for a long time, families with children, and people whose skin dries easily
Third-party certified eco detergentBesides the source of the ingredients, an international body audits the organic or eco content (for example ECOCERT). The bar is higher and the price is usually the highest, but there is outside proofPeople who care where the ingredients come from and want certification to back it up, not just packaging claims

No single type is "the best". It depends on how often you wash by hand, how sensitive your skin is, and how much extra you are willing to pay for certification. More certifications are not necessarily better; a more credible certification is preferable.

4 Things to Check When Choosing Dish Soap

What to checkWhat to look at
Ingredients and certificationWhether it has a third-party certification (such as ECOCERT, the EU natural and organic certification, or the SNQ National Quality Mark), and specific claims such as "LAS-free" or "formaldehyde-free". These are more useful than the vague word "natural"
ConcentrationFor the same volume, a more concentrated soap usually lasts longer. Work out the amount per use to see what is really worth it, not just the price of one bottle
PackagingA bottle is convenient to use directly. A refill pack is usually cheaper and uses less plastic, but work out whether the price per milliliter is really lower than the bottle. Some refill packs are just a smaller size and not always a better deal
What it can be used forSoaps labeled "food grade" or "for fruit, vegetables, and dishes" usually go through a stricter assessment of residue after rinsing. This matters more if you often wash fruit, vegetables, or baby bottles at home

Are Refill Packs Really Cheaper? How to Work It Out

As with tissue paper, do not compare dish soap by "how much for one bottle". Work out "total price ÷ volume (ML)" to get the cost per milliliter, so you can fairly compare the bottle and the refill pack. For example, if a 1000ML bottle costs $150 and an 800ML refill pack costs $95, the bottle works out to about $0.15 per ML and the refill pack to about $0.119 per ML, so the refill pack really is cheaper. But if the refill pack works out to a higher unit price, it may just be a smaller pack at a higher price. You only know once you have worked it out, so do not go by the price number on the label.

Choosing by Situation

  • You wash dishes by hand often and your skin dries easily — Look first at a natural or plant-based formula, or a gentle one with a third-party certification, to reduce irritation from long contact.
  • You have children, or often use it to wash baby bottles or fruit and vegetables — Check that it is labeled "food grade" or "for fruit, vegetables, and dishes", and confirm the recommended way to rinse.
  • Price comes first and you have many greasy pots — Ordinary synthetic soaps usually clean better, and you can add a scouring pad to help remove grease.
  • You buy it again and again and want to save money — Work out the price per ML first and check that the refill pack is really cheaper than the bottle, then decide whether to buy several at once.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an ECOCERT certification mean on a dish soap?
ECOCERT is a European certification body for natural and organic products. It audits whether the source of the ingredients and the eco content meet its thresholds, and not every dish soap applies for it. Having it usually means a third party has backed up the claims about ingredients and the environment, but the certification does not mean "cleans the strongest", so you still need to judge by your own cleaning needs.
Is a refill pack always cheaper than a bottle?
Not always. You have to work out "total price ÷ volume (ML)" to know. Some refill packs hold much less than the bottle, so the unit price after working it out is not necessarily lower. Before you order, use a calculator, and do not go only by the price number printed on the pack.
Can I use dish soap to wash fruit and vegetables?
Check whether the product is labeled "food grade" or "for fruit, vegetables, and dishes". An ordinary dish soap without this label has not necessarily been assessed for residue on items that come into contact with food. Products with the label are usually designed with the risk of residue after rinsing in mind, so they are better for washing produce.
