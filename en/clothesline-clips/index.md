# How to Choose a Clothesline with Clips (曬衣繩) in Taiwan: Stretch Cord, Rust-Resistant Clips, and Load Capacity

Last updated: October 24, 2026
選品索引 ChoiceIndex — https://choiceindex.tw/en/

When you have no drying rack in a rented room, a dorm, or on a trip, a stretch clothesline is a common substitute. But ordinary cotton cord, stretch cord, and stretch cord with built-in clips differ quite a lot, and whether the clips resist rust, whether they grip hard enough for heavy clothes, and how long the cord can stretch all affect how well it works. This guide compares the 3 cord-and-clip setups, explains 4 things to check, and shows how to work out the cost per clip so the number of clips does not mislead you, so you can decide which one to buy.

## First, Understand the 3 Cord-and-Clip Setups

| Type | Features | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary cotton / nylon cord (no clips) | Cheap but with no stretch, so it sags easily with heavy loads, and you have to prepare separate clothespins | People who put price first and only dry light, thin clothes |
| Stretch clothesline (no clips) | It stretches without sagging easily, and is quick to put up and take down, but you still have to buy clips separately and store them separately | People who often change where they dry, and care about how durable the cord is |
| Stretch clothesline with built-in clips | One line includes the stretch cord and several movable clips, so you do not need to buy clips, and you can move the clips freely | Rentals, dorms, travel, and people who want everything in one set |

No single type is "the best". It mainly depends on whether you have a fixed drying space, how heavy the clothes you usually dry are, and whether you are willing to spend time matching clips yourself. A set with built-in clips is convenient, but the clip material and grip strength vary, so choose with care.

## 4 Things to Check When Choosing a Clothesline and Clips

| What to check | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Clip material and rust resistance | See whether it says "stainless steel wire with a rubber coating". Indoor drying areas are often damp, and ordinary thin iron wire clips easily rust and stick with long use, and can even stain clothes |
| Grip strength and load | The spring clip needs to hold heavy clothes such as jeans, towels, and jackets. If the grip is too weak, clothes slide off in the middle of the night |
| How far it stretches | Look at both the length laid flat and the length when stretched, and measure the actual distance you want to put it up (such as across a balcony or a room diagonal) to see whether it is long enough |
| Anti-slip design | Types with positioning beads or anti-slip stoppers keep the clips from sliding to one side of the cord while you dry, and the cord is also less likely to shift |

## How to Work Out the Cost per Clip So the Number of Clips Does Not Fool You

Stretch clotheslines with built-in clips come in versions with 8, 10, 12 clips, and so on. Do not look only at "how much for one line". Work out "total price ÷ number of clips" for the cost per clip, so you can fairly compare different options. For example, one 10-clip set costs $49 and another 12-clip set costs $59, which works out to about $4.9 per clip for the first and about $4.9 for the second, so they are about the same value. But if the number of clips differs a lot and the total price hardly differs, also think about whether the cord length and the clip material have been cut back, and do not compare only the number of clips.

## Choosing by Situation

- **Rental, dorm, or drying clothes in a room** — Choose a type with positioning beads and a cord long enough to stretch across a diagonal, so ordinary iron clips do not rust and scratch walls or furniture.
- **Often taking it camping or traveling** — Choose one that is easy to store (it rolls up small) and has a light cord, because too many clips take up space and add weight.
- **Often drying heavy clothes (jeans, towels, jackets)** — Look first at whether the grip is strong enough and whether it is stainless steel, because ordinary thin iron clips can fail to hold and slip.
- **Drying small items such as socks at the same time** — More clips are enough, and you do not have to queue with large items for space.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will a stretch clothesline loosen over time so the clips do not grip?**
The stretch cord itself has a service life, and long exposure to sun and rain speeds up aging and loosening of rubber. If the clips are stainless steel wire with a rubber coating, they usually last longer than ordinary thin iron clips, but we still suggest using it indoors or in a shaded place and checking regularly that the grip is still strong enough. Once it loosens, replace it to keep clothes from falling.

**How long can a clothesline stretch, and will it snap?**
The "stretchable length" on a product page is the maker's test figure (for example 180 cm laid flat and stretching to 380 cm). In real use we suggest not stretching it to the maximum for a long time, because that speeds up fatigue of the elastic and can even break it. Using a length slightly shorter than the stated maximum makes the cord last longer and is also safer.

**Are more clips always better on a clothesline?**
Not necessarily, and it depends on what you dry. More clips let you hang more small items at once, but if you often dry large items (such as bath towels and jackets), too many clips take up space and are hard to arrange. We suggest working out "total price ÷ number of clips" to compare the unit price, and then deciding by the kinds of clothes you usually dry, because more is not always a better deal.
