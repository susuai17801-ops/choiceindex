How to Choose an Extension Cord (延長線) in Taiwan: Check the Inspection Mark, Rated Power, Overload Protection and Switches

Last updated: October 20, 2026

An extension cord lets you plug multiple devices into one wall outlet, but it does not increase the amount of power available. Whether it has three sockets or six, the total current you can use is limited by the rating printed on the cord, and also by the wall outlet and wiring behind it. Buying the wrong one, plugging in too much, or connecting a high-power appliance are common reasons an extension cord overheats or even starts a fire. This guide explains 5 things to check before you buy, safety rules for using it, and what to watch for if you brought devices from home and use a plug adapter together with an extension cord.

First, Understand: More Sockets Does Not Mean More Power

The rating on an extension cord (for example 15A, 1650W) is the limit for the whole cord added together, not for each socket separately. The labeled wattage of everything you use at the same time should not add up to more than the wattage printed on the cord. For high-power appliances such as heaters, hair dryers, electric kettles, rice cookers, and ovens, plug them directly into a wall outlet rather than an extension cord. An extension cord is meant for temporary, supplementary use, not as permanent wiring.

Safety When Using an Extension Cord

  • Keep the total within the rating — The labeled wattage of devices used at the same time should not add up to more than the wattage on the cord. Do not fill every socket either.
  • Do not connect high-power appliances — Heaters, hair dryers, electric kettles, rice cookers, ovens, and microwaves use a lot of power. Plug them directly into a wall outlet rather than an extension cord.
  • Do not chain extension cords — Do not plug one extension cord into another, or connect several in a row. The cables and outlet may carry more current than they can handle.
  • Do not use it coiled up — Pull the whole cord out straight when it is in use. A coiled cord under load can overheat. Do not run it under heavy objects, a door, or a carpet.
  • Plug in firmly, stop if it gets hot — A loose plug can heat up and even spark. If it feels hot, smells burnt, or the casing changes color or shape, unplug it right away and stop using it.
  • Keep away from damp places and children — Do not use it in a bathroom or next to a sink. Keep it out of reach of children and do not let pets chew on it.
  • For temporary use — An extension cord is for temporary, supplementary use. If you need many outlets for a long time, ask your employer, landlord, or a qualified electrician about adding outlets.

If You Brought Devices From Home

  • An extension cord does not change the voltage — Household outlets in Taiwan are 110V. If the INPUT range on a device label does not include 110V (for example, a label that says only 220V or 230V), do not use it just because the plug fits. Neither an extension cord nor a plug adapter can fix a voltage difference.
  • With an adapter and an extension cord, respect both limits — When you use a plug adapter together with an extension cord, the whole line can only handle what the weaker of the two can. If the adapter does not list a rated current and power, treat it as suitable only for low-power devices such as phone and laptop chargers, and do not connect high-power heating appliances.
  • Do not stack an adapter on an extension cord — Avoid stacking adapters and extension cords layer on layer. Use only one adapter at a time, and plug it directly into a wall outlet for a more secure connection.
  • Read your device's own label — Whether a device can be used with an extension cord, and whether several can be used together, depends on its label and manual. If you are unsure, ask your employer, landlord, or a qualified electrician instead of guessing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does more sockets mean I can use more electricity?
No. The rated current and power on the cord (for example 15A and 1650W) is the limit for the whole cord. More sockets only means more devices can be connected at once. The labeled wattage of devices used at the same time should not add up to more than the wattage on the cord.
Can I plug a heater, hair dryer, or rice cooker into an extension cord?
These devices use a lot of power, so it is better to plug them directly into a wall outlet. If you really have to, first check that the cord's rated power is higher than the device's power, and do not plug in other devices at the same time. If you are unsure, ask a qualified electrician.
Can I plug an extension cord into another extension cord?
It is not recommended. Chained cords and outlets may carry more current than they can handle and overheat. Use one cord that is long enough instead of connecting several in a row.
Can I use a plug adapter together with an extension cord?
Low-power chargers (phones and laptops whose label includes 110V in the INPUT range) can be used, but you must respect both limits. If the adapter does not list a rated current and power, treat it as suitable only for low-power devices, and do not connect high-power heating appliances. Also do not stack adapters layer on layer on an extension cord.
What should I do if the extension cord gets hot or smells burnt?
Unplug it right away and stop using it. Possible reasons include too many devices or a device that uses too much power, a loose plug with poor contact, or an old or poor-quality cable. Do not keep using it, and do not open it yourself.
What does "overload protection" mean?
It is a feature that cuts the power automatically when the current goes above the rated value. It is one of the conditions worth looking for when you buy, but it does not mean you can use more than the rating, so you still need to keep an eye on the total power.
