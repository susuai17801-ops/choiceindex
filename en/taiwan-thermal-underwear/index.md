# Thermal Underwear in Taiwan: How to Read the Labels and Heat Claims, and How to Layer Thin and Thick Pieces When the Weather Keeps Changing (發熱衣)

Last updated: November 25, 2026
選品索引 ChoiceIndex — https://choiceindex.tw/en/

Taiwan's winter is often cold in the morning and evening and warm at midday, and the north can be damp and cold. Thermal underwear is priced from about a hundred to a few hundred NT dollars, labeled “graphene”, “far-infrared”, or “moisture-to-heat”, but from the look and feel of the fabric it is hard to tell whether it is really warm. This page covers three things: what the media report says about New Taipei consumer-protection officers' tests of thermal wear, which garment labels to check, and how to layer thin and thick pieces when the weather changes. At the end are two picks: a thin long-sleeve shirt and a thicker graphene one (the same product page also has a vest style).

## What did the consumer-protection officers' tests find

According to a NOWnews report dated November 14, 2024, New Taipei City's consumer-protection officers tested 12 thermal garments on the market before winter: all 12 did generate heat, but the temperature rise varied a lot; 4 showed a rise lower than the seller's claim, and 4 had an average and maximum rise below the industry standard (the report gives an average rise of 0.8℃ or more and a maximum rise of 2.5℃ or more); no formaldehyde was found in any of the 12, but 11 had labels that did not meet the rules. The report says the tests followed FTTS-FA-023 “moisture absorption and heat release” and FTTS-FA-010 “far-infrared emissivity”, and some sellers said the report on their site was for the yarn rather than the finished garment, or was several years old. The report also says the best performer was also the most expensive item in that test.

This is a media report; we did not find the original announcement from the New Taipei government. The result is from 2024, does not mean every product today is the same, and the sample was only 12 items. We did not match our two recommendations against that list, so we cannot say whether either was or was not tested. A more practical approach: when buying, see whether the seller provides a test report for the finished garment, rather than relying only on the word “graphene”.

## How to read garment labels

Under the Bureau of Standards, Metrology and Inspection's (BSMI) textile labeling standard, garments carry several label items. The table below is our summary:

| Label | Content | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber content | For example polyester or elastic fiber, with the name and weight percentage | Fibers making up 5% or more of the garment must be listed by name and weight percentage, with a tolerance of ±3%. No tolerance is allowed for items marked 100%. BSMI's labeling examples list using only an informal name such as “microfiber” as a common mistake. “Graphene” is the seller's material claim; we did not find an official explanation for thermal wear. |
| Country of origin | Country of manufacture | Should be on the hang tag or the garment; if the product page says made in Taiwan, also check the label on what you receive. |
| Size | Size, or a height-and-weight chart | Sellers' size charts are measured by hand and can be off; both sellers remind you to check the size first. |
| Washing and ironing | Care-label symbols | Decides whether it can be dried or bleached; go by the care label. |
| Manufacturer or importer | Name, phone, address; imported goods must also give the foreign manufacturer's name in its own script | Look on the hang tag; if the online page does not say, ask the seller before ordering. |

## How to check the heat claims

- **Is the report for the garment or the yarn** — In the report, some sellers said the test on their site was for heat-generating yarn, not the finished garment; yarn or fabric data is not necessarily the result for a sewn garment.
- **Check the year of the report and the color of the tested garment** — Some sellers also said the report was several years old, or that the tested garment was deep black. If the style you buy does not match the report, treat the numbers as a reference only.
- **Check whether there are numbers** — A claim such as “extra warm” with no numbers cannot be checked; if there is a temperature-rise figure, see which standard it was measured under.
- **Graphene and double-sided velvet are material names** — We found no official statement that graphene is definitely warmer or that double-sided velvet is definitely warmer, so this page does not quote such claims.

## When the weather keeps changing, how to layer thin and thick pieces (our summary)

This is our summary, not official advice, and we have not tested it: in Taiwan's winter the gap between day and night, and between indoors and outdoors, can be large, and a very thick garment can be stuffy at midday. A more flexible way is two layers: a thin one next to the skin, then add a thick piece or a vest in the morning, evening, or cold weather, and take one off when it is warm. The two products on this page can be combined that way.

- **A thin long sleeve as the base** — The seller describes the A-Yao one as a thin long-sleeve shirt, suitable as the first layer and easy to wear under a jacket.
- **Add a thick piece or a vest** — The seller describes the M.F one as made of thick, lightly brushed fabric, and the same product page also has long-sleeve and vest styles; add it when it is cold. A vest does not cover the arms, so it is easier to wear over or under a long sleeve without feeling tight.
- **Do not buy too tight** — When layering, the base layer only needs to fit snugly; if the outer layer is too tight it will press on the inner one. Check the size charts of both products.

## What to look at when buying thermal underwear

- **Content and origin clearly stated** — Does the product page give the fiber content, origin, and care label? The specification fields of both products give material and origin, but no percentage breakdown, and we did not see a care label in the text.
- **Heat claims with numbers and a report** — If there is a temperature-rise figure or test result, check whether it is for the garment or the yarn, which year, and which standard. Claims without numbers are the seller's word.
- **Style options clearly stated** — Men's and women's, long sleeve, vest, and thickness are often in one product page; confirm which style you picked before ordering.
- **Return rules for underwear** — Clothing worn against the skin counts as a personal hygiene item, and sellers often write that it cannot be returned once opened; read the return rules before ordering.
