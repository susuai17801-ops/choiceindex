## First, Understand the 4 Things to Check on a Home LED Bulb

| What to check | What to look at | A common problem |
|---|---|---|
| Base type | The base decides whether the bulb fits. The most common in Taiwan homes are E27 (the larger screw base) and E14 (the smaller screw base), and there are also bayonet, GU10, and other types. Before you buy, check the markings on the existing socket or the bottom of the old bulb | If the base does not match, it will not fit even if the brightness and color temperature are right, and this is the easiest thing to get wrong |
| Color temperature | Shown in K (kelvin). The lower the number, the warmer and more yellow the light, and the higher the number, the cooler and whiter it looks. There are three common ones: warm light about 3000K, natural light about 4000K, and white light about 6500K, and you should go by the shop page and the pack | Mixing different color temperatures in one space looks uneven |
| Brightness | Look at lumens (lm) rather than watts (W). For an LED, the watts are only the power it uses, and brightness is shown by lumens. Different products with the same watts can have different lumens | Buying by watts alone may give you something dimmer or brighter than the old bulb |
| Labels and safety | See whether the shop page shows an energy-saving label, an eco label, and a BSMI inspection number. Having a label does not mean it suits your fixture, so you still need to check the scope of use and the voltage label | Closed shades, dimmer switches, and damp places such as a bathroom need a separate check on whether it suits |

No single color temperature or brightness is "the best". It depends on which space it goes in and what it is used for. A common approach in ordinary homes is to use warm or natural light in the living room and bedroom for relaxing, natural or white light in the study, kitchen, and bathroom where you need to see clearly, and white light in hallways and balconies, which are more practical. If the existing fixture states a maximum wattage, cannot be dimmed, or is enclosed, check that before you buy. This page only explains how to choose and is not product safety advice. Before you buy, go by the scope of use stated on the pack and the shop page.

## Where the 3 Common Color Temperatures Suit

| Color temperature | How it feels | Common use |
|---|---|---|
| Warm light (about 3000K) | Warm and soft, with a relaxing mood, but colors are harder to tell apart | Living room, bedroom, over the dining table, and spaces where you want a warm mood |
| Natural light (about 4000K) | Between warm and white, not too cold and not too yellow, and very general-purpose | Study, kitchen, hallway, and a common middle choice when you are not sure which to pick |
| White light (about 6500K) | Cooler and bright, so you can see clearly, but looking at it for a long time may feel harsh | Bathroom, kitchen counter, balcony, garage, and places where you need bright light to work |

## What to Note When Changing a Bulb

- **Turn off the power and let the bulb cool** — Switch it off before you change it. A bulb that has just been turned off is very hot, so wait until it cools before touching it, and keep your hands dry and do not change a bulb in a damp place.
- **Match the base and length of the old bulb** — Take the old bulb with you or photograph the markings on the bottom. Besides the base, also check whether there is enough room in the shade, because a tall globe bulb can catch on the shade.
- **Check dimmable and enclosed fixtures** — An ordinary LED bulb cannot always be used with a dimmer switch, and on a dimmer it may flicker. In an enclosed shade, heat escapes poorly and this can shorten its life, so see whether the shop page says it suits.
- **Use the same color temperature in one space** — Mixing warm and white light in one room or one row of lights makes the brightness feel uneven, so when you change, change them all to the same type at once.
- **Treat the lifespan figure on a shop page as a reference only** — The hours shown are test values under specific conditions, and the real life is affected by how often you switch it on and off, heat, and voltage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I convert the watts of an LED bulb directly from an old bulb?**
We do not recommend going only by watts. For an LED the watts are the power it uses, and brightness is shown by lumens. Before you buy, look at the old bulb's lumens, or compare using the lumens on the shop page, and then decide whether to change to an LED with the same watts.

**What is the difference between E27 and E14?**
Both are screw bases. E27 is larger and is the most common size for home globe bulbs, while E14 is smaller and is common in small pendant lights, wall lights, and some table lamps. The number is the diameter of the screw base in millimeters, and the two are not interchangeable.

**White, natural, or warm light: which should I choose?**
There is no standard answer. Warm light is preferred for relaxing and bedtime spaces, natural light is often used for general purposes, the study, and the kitchen, and white light is often chosen for bathrooms and balconies where you need to see clearly. If you are not sure, buy one bulb and try it in its place for a few days before changing them all.

**Can an LED bulb be used in a bathroom or outdoors?**
It depends on whether the shop page and the pack say it suits damp or outdoor places, because an ordinary indoor bulb cannot always be used there. In a bathroom, also check that the fixture itself is moisture-proof.
