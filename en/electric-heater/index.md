## What to Do After You Get It (From BSMI's Use Tips)

- **Read the manual first** — Before use, read the method in the manual and pay special attention to the warnings it lists.
- **Do not use it in a closed space** — BSMI says some heater designs consume oxygen, so keep the room properly ventilated to avoid a lack of oxygen.
- **Do not cover it, and keep it away from flammable things** — Never cover the heater with cloth or clothes to dry them, and keep a suitable distance from flammable things, especially wallpaper and curtains. When sleeping, do not point the heater straight at the quilt beside the bed.
- **Use a dedicated socket** — An electric heater uses a lot of power, so use a dedicated socket and do not share the same socket group with other appliances.
- **Watch the air outlet and the filter** — The air outlet releases hot air, so keep people from touching it and children from playing near it. Also clean the filter regularly so dust and lint do not build up.
- **Send it for repair if it breaks** — If there is a fault, send it to the maker's designated repair center and never take it apart yourself.
- **Follow the manual** — This page only collects BSMI's tips and does not give safety advice, so for actual use go by the product manual.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does an electric heater need the commodity inspection mark?**
BSMI Taichung Branch's "Notes on Using Electric Heaters" (published September 16, 2025) says electric heaters are already listed as products that must be inspected, so when you buy you should check that the commodity inspection mark is attached. BSMI's "Electrical Appliance Safety" notice also says that electrical products without the approval mark are usually products that skipped inspection, and their safety has not been confirmed by standard tests. The lookup address for the mark is civil.bsmi.gov.tw/bsmi_pqn/. Follow the official system for the exact steps.

**Can I plug an electric heater into an extension cord?**
BSMI Taichung Branch advises using a dedicated socket for an electric heater and not sharing the same socket group with other appliances. BSMI also notes that an ordinary socket or extension cord has a maximum safe current capacity of only 15 amperes, and using high-power appliances such as electric heaters together often overloads the wiring and causes fires. We did not find an official statement on whether any particular extension cord is suitable for an electric heater, and we do not give safety advice, so go by the manuals and labels of the heater and the extension cord.

**Can I leave it on all night or use it while sleeping?**
BSMI's use tips give no time rule for sleeping use. They only advise following the manual, keeping the room ventilated, and not covering the heater with clothes, and they say that when sleeping you should not point the heater straight at the quilt beside the bed. We do not give safety advice, so go by the product manual on whether it suits overnight use.
