How to Choose Disposable Hand Warmers (暖暖包) in Taiwan: Stick-On, Hand-Held, Foot Warmers, and How to Avoid Low-Temperature Burns

Last updated: October 18, 2026

When the weather in Taiwan turns cold, disposable hand warmers (暖暖包, nuǎnnuǎn bāo) are useful for commuting, working on your feet, caring for older people, or going out. On Shopee you will find stick-on warmers that attach to clothing, hand-held ones, foot warmers, and labels such as 8, 12, or 15 hours of heat, so it is hard to know what to pick. Hand warmers look simple, but their temperature is often above 50°C. If you stick one directly on your skin or use it while sleeping, you can get a low-temperature burn. This guide explains the 4 types, 5 things to check before you buy, and what you must pay attention to when using them.

There is no single "best" hand warmer. It depends on where you use it, for how long, and who is using it. For commuting or standing work, a stick-on type worn over clothing works well. For quick hand warming, the hand-held type is faster. If your feet get cold, add foot warmers. If it is for an older person, a person with diabetes, or someone who stays in bed, the risk is higher than for other people, so read "Safety When Using Hand Warmers" below first. This page is only a buying guide, not medical advice. A hand warmer does not replace warm clothing and is not for treating illness. If you feel unwell, see a doctor.

Safety When Using Hand Warmers (Low-Temperature Burns)

  • Do not stick it directly on the skin — A hand warmer is often hotter than 50°C. A low-temperature burn happens when the skin is in contact with a moderate temperature (about 44 to 50°C) for a long time. It may not hurt much at the time, and redness, swelling, or blisters appear later. Always use it over clothing.
  • Do not use it while sleeping — During sleep the skin is less sensitive to heat, the body can easily press on the same spot, and the warmer can get hotter than usual. It is not recommended to use it while sleeping.
  • Check and move it regularly — While using it, check the skin and move it to another spot regularly (doctors often suggest every 20 to 30 minutes). If the skin turns red, stings, or feels uncomfortably hot, take it off right away.
  • People with diabetes, older people, and people who stay in bed need extra care — People with reduced sensation can be burned without noticing, and may find it harder to say they are uncomfortable. If you use a hand warmer for such a person, check the skin together. Do not stick it on and walk away.
  • Do not tie it tightly or press on it — Tying a hand warmer to the body with a strap, or sitting or lying on it, stops the heat from escaping and increases the risk of a burn.
  • If you already have a burn — Remove the hand warmer, rinse with cool running water to lower the temperature, and do not pop blisters yourself. If the injury is serious or large, see a doctor.
  • Throwing it away after use — A used warmer may still be warm. Let it cool completely, then dispose of it according to your local waste sorting rules. Do not open the package, and keep it out of reach of children and pets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between stick-on and hand-held hand warmers?
A stick-on warmer has adhesive and sticks to clothing. The heat usually lasts longer but it warms up more slowly. A hand-held warmer heats up when you open it and shake it, so it suits quick hand warming, but its high-heat time is usually shorter. Choose according to whether you use it on the body or on the hands.
Can I stick a hand warmer directly on my skin?
No. A hand warmer is often hotter than 50°C, so sticking it directly on the skin, or leaving it on the same spot for a long time, risks a low-temperature burn. Stick it on the outside of clothing and check the skin regularly.
Can I sleep with a hand warmer on?
It is not recommended. During sleep you feel heat less, the body easily presses on the same spot, and the warmer can get hotter than usual, so you can be burned without noticing.
Do I need to rub the hand warmer after opening it?
No. Shops and packages usually explain that after opening you only need to shake it a few times and it heats up slowly. Rubbing too much can make the iron powder heat unevenly and shorten the heating time. Follow the instructions on the package.
What should I do if I get a low-temperature burn?
Remove the hand warmer, rinse with cool running water, and do not pop blisters yourself. If the skin is red and painful, has blisters, or the area is large, see a doctor. We do not give medical advice.
Can people with diabetes or older people use hand warmers?
They may have reduced sensation, so they are less likely to notice a burn, and the risk is higher than for other people. If they do use one, ask someone to help check the skin, use it over clothing and only for a short time, and ask a doctor or caregiver first.
