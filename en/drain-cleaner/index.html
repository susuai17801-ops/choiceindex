How to Choose Drain Cleaner (水管疏通劑) in Taiwan: Gel, Liquid, Powder, and Drain Plungers Compared

Last updated: October 24, 2026

A bathroom sink that drains slowly, a kitchen sink that holds water, and a floor drain that starts to smell are mostly caused by hair, grease, and soap scum building up in the pipe. In the store there are gel, liquid, and powder drain cleaners, with claims such as "unclogs in 15 minutes" and "dissolves instantly", but each type is used differently, suits different pipes, and has different cautions, and using the wrong one can even damage pipes or be dangerous. This guide explains the 4 ways to deal with a clog, 4 things to check, and what to read before using it, so you can decide which one to buy.

First, Understand the 4 Common Ways to Deal with a Clogged Drain

TypeFeaturesWho it is for
Gel drain cleanerA thicker texture that sinks to the bottom of the pipe and clings to the clog, so it is less easily diluted by standing water. You usually pour it in, leave it for a while, and then flush with plenty of waterA drain that holds water or runs slowly, where you want the product to work right on the clog
Liquid drain cleanerA texture like ordinary liquid that is easy to pour. In a pipe with standing water it may be diluted and work less well. Depending on the product there are strong alkaline types, chlorine types, and so on, so read the label carefullyA pipe that is not fully blocked yet, and people who want regular upkeep to prevent clogs
Powder / foaming drain cleanerThe powder produces foam or heat when water is added and breaks down dirt through that reaction. You usually follow the water amount and steps on the pack, which is one more step than a liquidA drain clogged with grease and hair, for people who are happy to follow the steps
Plunger / drain snake (no chemicals)Uses hand pressure or a drain snake to push or hook out the clog, with no contact with strong alkali or acid. For a clog of hair tangled into a mass, it is often more effective than chemicalsPeople who want to avoid chemicals, have old pipes, or are not sure of the pipe material

No single type is "the best". It mainly depends on where the clog is, the product ingredients, and the pipe material in your home. For a drain that is slow but still flowing, a chemical product is usually enough. If it is fully blocked and the water just sits, a chemical sitting in the water works poorly, and a strong product staying in the pipe can add strain to it, so a plunger or drain snake is usually safer. This page only explains how to choose and is not plumbing work or product safety advice. If it keeps clogging, we suggest having a professional plumber check.

4 Things to Check When Choosing Drain Cleaner

What to checkWhat to look at
Does the type match the clog?First look at which drain it is and how long it has been blocked. Hair clogs in a sink or bathroom and grease clogs in a kitchen suit different approaches. A blocked toilet is a different situation, and many drain cleaners clearly state they are not for toilets, so check the stated scope before ordering
Scope of use and pipe materialLook at whether the shop page and pack say which drains it suits, such as kitchen, bathroom, and sink. Old pipes, special-material pipes, and metal fittings tolerate strong alkaline or acidic products differently. If you are not sure of the material, check the pack instructions or ask a professional, and do not just pour it in
How to use it, how long to wait, and how to rinseMost products say to pour in, leave for about ten to thirty minutes, and then flush with plenty of water. Some say cold water and some say hot water, and this differs a lot between products. Follow the pack, and do not add water, heat it, or mix it with other cleaners yourself
How complete the labels and warnings areCheck whether the pack or shop page clearly gives the ingredients, hazard warnings, first aid, and storage. Strong products usually require gloves and good ventilation, and should not come into contact with the eyes or skin. Labels on a shop page are mostly the seller's description, and this site has not checked them one by one

What to Remember When Using Drain Cleaner

  • Never mix with other cleaners, vinegar, alcohol, or bleach — Mixing a drain cleaner with an acidic cleaner or chlorine bleach can produce harmful gas. Use only one at a time, rinse well with water before switching to another, and keep the room ventilated.
  • Wear gloves, and avoid contact with eyes and skin — A strong drain cleaner can irritate or hurt skin and eyes. Do not squeeze the bottle when opening it, to avoid splashes. If it gets on your skin or in your eyes, rinse with plenty of clean water, and see a doctor if you feel unwell.
  • Keep it off clothes, carpet, and wooden items — Some products can fade fabric or wood. Aim at the drain when pouring and do not spill around it. If it spills, wipe it up and rinse right away.
  • Keep out of reach of children and pets — Keep it in its original container and do not pour it into another one. Store it in a cool place, away from direct sunlight and heat, and out of reach of children and pets.
  • Results differ, and if it keeps clogging find the cause — How fast and how well it works, as stated on a shop page, is mostly the seller's description or users' experience. The real result depends on what is blocking the pipe and the condition of the pipe, and there is no guarantee. If the same drain keeps clogging, the pipe itself may be the problem, so have a professional plumber check.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will drain cleaner damage pipes?
It depends on the ingredients and strength. Strong alkaline or acidic products can affect old pipes, special-material pipes, or metal fittings, especially if the product sits in the pipe for a long time. "Does not harm pipes" on a shop page is mostly the seller's description, so first read the scope and the time stated on the pack, and if you are not sure of the pipe material, ask a professional.
If the water is fully standing and not draining, does pouring in drain cleaner help?
Usually the effect is limited. A product poured into standing water is easily diluted and does not reach the clog well, and the standing water makes the product stay in the pipe longer. In this case, you can first try a plunger or drain snake, or ask a professional plumber to check. Always follow the product instructions.
Can drain cleaner be used with vinegar, baking soda, or other cleaners?
We do not recommend it. Mixing a drain cleaner with an acidic cleaner, vinegar, alcohol, or chlorine bleach can produce harmful gas or a violent reaction. Use only one at a time, rinse well with water before switching to another, and keep the room ventilated. For details, see the notes on the product pack.
Can drain cleaner be used for a blocked toilet?
Not necessarily. Many drain cleaners clearly say on the pack or shop page that they are not for toilets, because a toilet is built differently and drains differently. Before ordering, check the scope stated on the product, and if it does not suit a toilet, do not pour it in, and ask a professional if needed.
What should I be careful about with drain cleaner if I have children or pets at home?
Most drain cleaners are strong cleaning products. Keep them in the original container, out of reach of children and pets, and do not let them near the drain while you use it. If it gets on skin or in eyes, rinse with plenty of clean water and see a doctor. If it is swallowed, act as the pack says and see a doctor as soon as you can.
