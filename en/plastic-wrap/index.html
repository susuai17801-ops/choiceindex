How to Choose Plastic Wrap (保鮮膜) in Taiwan: PE, PVC, and PVDC Compared, and Microwave and Fridge Use

Last updated: October 24, 2026

Whether plastic wrap fails to cling, sticks to food in the microwave, tangles when you cut it, or leaves you unsure whether you can cover hot food with it depends largely on the material and how you use it. On the shop page there is a lot about PE, PVC, PVDC, "food grade", and "heat resistant", but each material differs in cling, barrier, and heat range, and suits different things to wrap. This guide explains the differences between the 3 common materials, 4 things to check, and what to remember when using it, so you can decide which one to buy.

First, Understand the 3 Common Materials of Household Plastic Wrap

MaterialFeaturesWho it is for
PE (polyethylene)A softer film with weaker cling than the other materials, and generally thought to have a limited heat range. It is often labeled as usable in the microwave and is usually the more affordable one. The seal and barrier are average, so it suits short-term fridge storage or covering a bowlCovering bowls, wrapping fruit and vegetables, short-term fridge storage, and people on a budget
PVC (polyvinyl chloride)Good clarity and cling, so it wraps snugly and pulls apart easily. It is generally thought to have a limited heat range, some types contain plasticizers, and a common suggestion is to keep it away from high heat and very fatty food and not to microwave it directly. Formulas differ by brand, so read the packWrapping cold food, fridge storage, and people who need a snug wrap
PVDC (polyvinylidene chloride)Common in Japan and some imported brands. It clings strongly and has a better barrier against moisture and oxygen, so it suits food stored in the fridge or freezer for a long time. It usually costs more than PE, and the microwave and heat range depend on each brand's labelPeople who store food in the fridge or freezer for a long time and care about freshness

No single type is "the best". It depends on what you are wrapping, how long it will be kept, and whether it will go in the microwave. A common approach in ordinary homes is to use PE for covering bowls and wrapping fruit, to consider PVDC only for food that needs a snug wrap or long storage, and for hot or very fatty food to use a lidded container or baking paper instead of putting wrap right against it. This page only explains how to choose and is not product safety advice. Before you buy, go by the scope of use stated on the pack and the shop page.

4 Things to Check When Choosing Plastic Wrap

What to checkWhat to look at
Material and labelFirst look at whether the pack or shop page clearly says PE, PVC, or PVDC. If it is not clear, avoid using it in the microwave or on very fatty food. Words such as "food grade" and "heat resistant" are mostly the maker's description, and you should read them together with the material and heat label
Use and heatingIf you want to wrap food for the microwave, check that the pack clearly says it can go in the microwave, and keep the film away from the food as the instructions say. Before wrapping hot or fatty food, check the heat and use range on the pack, and if it does not say, do not put it right against the food to heat it
Cling and how easy it is to pullStrong cling wraps well, but wrap that is too clingy tangles. Look at the cutting box design, how easy it is to pull out, and whether it rolls back, because these affect how smooth it is in daily use, and people who have bought it usually mention them in reviews
Length, width, and countWhether you usually wrap bowls, fruit, or large cuts of food decides whether you want a wide or narrow roll. A big bulk roll suits homes that use a lot, and a small roll suits occasional use or little space. Before you buy, divide the total price by the length, and think about how long it will take you to use it up, so it does not sit and get old

What to Remember When Using and Storing Wrap

  • Before microwaving, check that the pack says it can go in the microwave — Not every wrap suits the microwave. If you use it, follow the pack, keep the film away from the food, and leave a vent so steam does not build up under the film.
  • Do not heat hot or very fatty food with wrap right against it — Fat can get much hotter than water, and film against it can easily warp or break. For this kind of food, a lidded heat-resistant container is more reliable.
  • Plastic wrap is for one-time use — Throw away the part you pulled out after you use it, and do not wash and reuse it. Keep the rest of the roll in a dry, cool place away from heat.
  • Wrap raw and cooked food separately — Do not use wrap that has covered raw meat or fish for cooked or ready-to-eat food, and keep raw and cooked food apart in the fridge too.
  • Wrapped food does not mean it keeps for a long time — Wrap only reduces moisture loss and smells passing between foods. You still need to check the food's own shelf life separately. Eat leftovers soon, and do not eat food that smells bad or has gone bad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can plastic wrap go straight into the microwave?
It depends on whether the pack clearly says it can. Even a microwave-safe type should be used as the instructions say: keep the film away from the food and leave a vent, and for fatty or very hot food a lidded heat-resistant container is more reliable. If the label is not clear, we suggest not microwaving it.
What is the difference between PE and PVDC plastic wrap?
PE is softer, with weaker cling, and usually more affordable, and suits covering bowls and wrapping fruit day to day. PVDC clings strongly and has a better barrier against moisture and oxygen, is often used when food will be kept in the fridge or freezer for a long time, and usually costs more. The scope of use and heat labels differ by brand, so still go by the pack.
If it says "food grade" or "heat resistant", can I cover hot food with it?
Not necessarily. Such words are mostly the maker's description and do not mean every food and temperature is fine. Check the material and heat range on the pack, and for very fatty food we still suggest a lidded heat-resistant container, and not letting the film touch it while heating.
Can I wash and reuse the part of plastic wrap I have used?
We do not recommend it. Plastic wrap is for one-time use, and the part you pulled out that has touched food is more hygienic to throw away, and this also avoids tears and leftover smells.
