Adult diapers are not like tissue paper: if you buy the wrong one it is hard to return, because the size may not fit, the style may be hard to put on, and an opened pack usually cannot be returned. Everything on the package is in Chinese, and the same size label such as "L" can cover a different range in a different product line. This guide explains 5 things to check when you choose, how the common styles compare, and what to confirm before you order when you are shopping for an elderly person or your employer's household. It covers buying only and does not give medical or caregiving advice. If you have questions, ask a nurse, doctor, or caregiver.

## First Decide: Pull-Up Pants or Tape Style

A common way to choose is to ask whether the person can stand up and walk by themselves. People who can stand and move around usually choose pull-up pants (called 拉拉褲 or 復健褲 in Chinese), which they can put on and take off like underwear. People who stay in bed for long periods and need someone to change them usually choose the tape style (腰貼式), which can be changed while the person lies in bed. For light, occasional leaks in someone who is still active, some people use a pad inside regular underwear. Which one is right depends on the actual situation of the person who will wear it, so ask a nurse, doctor, or caregiver.

## Comparing Common Types

| Type | Features | Who it is for | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pull-up pants (拉拉褲 / 復健褲) | Like underwear: pulled up from the feet, usually with an elastic waist | People who can stand, walk, or get up with some help | Check the waist and hip size; harder to put on while lying down |
| Tape style (腰貼式) | Opened flat and fastened with tapes at both sides, so it can be changed in bed | People who stay in bed for long periods and need someone to change them | A crooked or loose tape can leak; size is based mainly on waist |
| Pad used with underwear (漏尿墊) | A pad is placed in the underwear, and only the pad is changed | Light leaks in a person who is still active | Limited absorbency, not the same as a diaper; choose the right size and length |
| Long-hours / night type | Higher absorbency and taller leak guards | People who wear it at night or for long hours | Thicker and warmer, not always suitable for an active day |

## Where People Most Often Buy the Wrong One

- **Looking only at S / M / L, without measuring** — The same "L" label can cover a different waist range in a different product line. Measure the waist and hip first, then compare with the size chart on the shop's product page.
- **Mixing up diapers and bed pads (中單)** — What you wear is a diaper or a pad inside underwear; what you lay on a bed or chair is a bed pad (also called 中單 or 看護墊). These terms are often used interchangeably, so check the product photos and specifications before you order.
- **Buying too much at once** — If you are not sure the size fits, buy a small pack or a single line first. Many shops say an opened pack cannot be returned.
- **Not separating day and night types** — A night type can be too thick for an active day, and a day type may not be enough at night. Choose by the time of use.
- **Not asking which type is currently used** — When you shop for an elderly person, first ask the family or your employer whether they use pull-up or tape style, which size, and which absorbency.

## If You Are Shopping for an Elderly Person or Your Employer's Household

- **Look at what is used now** — Take photos of the front and back of the package: brand, product line, size (S / M / L / XL), and number of pieces. Buying the same item is the safest way to avoid mistakes.
- **Know where to find the Chinese labels** — On the product page or package, look for these fields: "適用腰圍" (suitable waist), "尺寸" (size), "片數" (number of pieces), and "日用 / 夜用 / 長時間" (day / night / long hours). ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I choose pull-up pants or tape style?**
In general, people who can stand and walk by themselves usually choose pull-up pants (拉拉褲 or 復健褲), which they put on like underwear. People who stay in bed for long periods and need someone to change them usually choose tape style. Which one is right depends on the wearer's situation, so ask a nurse, doctor, or caregiver.

**How do I choose the size?**
Measure the waist and hips first, then compare with the size chart on the shop's product page or on the package. Do not guess from S / M / L, because even for the same brand the same label "L" can cover a different range in a different product line, so check the chart for the line you chose.

**What is the difference between a diaper, a pad, and a bed pad?**
A diaper is a complete product worn on the body. A pad (漏尿墊) is placed inside underwear and has limited absorbency. A bed pad (看護墊 or 中單) is laid on a bed or chair and is not worn. Shop listings often mix these words, so check the photos and specifications before you order.

**What is the difference between day and night types?**
Night or long-hours types usually have higher absorbency and taller leak guards, but they are also thicker. For an active day, a thin, easy-to-move-in type may be more suitable. Choose by the time of use.

**Can I return it if it does not fit?**
Many shops list adult diapers as personal consumable items to be opened only after you are sure you need them, and an opened pack usually cannot be returned. Read the shop's return policy before you order, and buy a small pack first if you are not sure of the size.

**What should I prepare when I shop for an elderly person?**
Look at the package they use now: take photos of the brand, product line, size, and number of pieces, and buying the same item is the safest way to avoid mistakes. If you are unsure of the style, size, or absorbency, ask your employer, the family, or a caregiver first instead of guessing.
