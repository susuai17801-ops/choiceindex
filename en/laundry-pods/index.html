How to Choose Laundry Pods (洗衣球) in Taiwan: Concentration, Ingredients, and Cost per Pod

Last updated: October 20, 2026

Laundry pods (capsules) need no measuring, you just drop them in the drum, and they are easier to store than a big bottle of detergent. But regular, concentrated, and functional types (anti-bacterial and anti-dust-mite, odor removal, softening) differ, and packs that look the same price can differ several times in cost per pod once you work it out. This guide explains 3 types, 4 things to check, and how to calculate whether a pack is really good value, so you can decide which one to buy.

First, Understand the 3 Types of Laundry Pods

TypeFeaturesWho it is for
Regular pods / capsulesA single-layer film, with enough cleaning power for everyday dirt, and usually the lowest price per podPeople who put budget first and have normally dirty clothes
Concentrated typePromotes concentrated cleaning ingredients (makers often claim "several times concentrated"). Each pod is small, but makers claim the cleaning effect is not reduced, and each pod usually costs morePeople who want to save storage space and wash large loads regularly
Functional type (anti-bacterial and anti-dust-mite / odor removal / softening)Extra claims such as anti-bacterial, anti-dust-mite, long-lasting scent, or softness, with more ingredients, and usually the highest pricePeople with allergies, pets or young children at home, or who care about how clothes smell

No type is "best". It depends on how dirty your clothes are, how sensitive the skin of your family is, and how much extra you are willing to pay for added functions. The concentration multiples that makers claim are marketing numbers, not a common verified standard, so do not use them as the only basis for comparison.

4 Things to Check When Choosing Laundry Pods

What to checkWhat to look at
Ingredients and cleaning technologyLook for specific ingredient labels such as natural soap liquid and enzymes. Numbers such as "several times concentrated" and "6X cleaning technology" are mostly the maker's internal tests or marketing claims, not a common third-party verified standard. What can support a claim is a clear ingredient list and a certification mark, not a multiple slogan
Suitable fabricsMost list cotton, linen, synthetic fibers, and blends. For delicate fabrics such as silk and wool, wash by hand with a neutral detergent or send them out, and do not simply use pods
Dose and washing machine typeTop-loading and front-loading machines mostly both work, but the number of pods for the water level and the weight of the load differs (for example, 1 pod for up to 50L of water and up to 4 kg of clothes, and 2 pods above that). Too few may not clean well, and too many may leave residue or waste
Cost per podBoth "concentrated" and "box is better value" packs differ a lot in count and price, so work out "total price ÷ number of pods" to see whether it is really good value. Do not go only by the price of the whole pack

How to Work Out the Cost per Pod

As with other household supplies, do not look only at "how much for one pack". Work out "total price ÷ number of pods" to get the cost per pod, so that you can compare different packs fairly. For example, a regular pack of 30 pods costs $300, which is $10 per pod. A family box of 150 pods costs $700, which is about $4.67 per pod, so the box is usually better value. But note that a "concentrated" type may recommend 2 pods per wash to match the effect of 1 regular pod. Count "how many pods you really use per wash" in the cost as well. If you only compare the price per pod, the apparent saving can be an illusion.

Choosing by Situation

  • Children with allergies, or pets, at home — First look at functional types that list their ingredients clearly (such as natural soap liquid and no specific irritants) with an anti-bacterial and anti-dust-mite claim, and do not order just because the word "anti-bacterial" is there.
  • Large loads and regular washing — A concentrated box usually has a lower cost per pod, but first check whether you still need 2 pods per wash.
  • Budget first, ordinary dirt — A regular type usually handles it, so there is no need to believe concentration multiples or function claims.
  • Sensitive to scent, or allergy-prone — Choose an unscented or low-scent type. The microcapsule slow-release technology in long-lasting scent types keeps fragrance on the skin for longer, so people with sensitive skin should take care.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are laundry pods different from liquid detergent or powder?
A pod (capsule) seals concentrated detergent in a water-soluble film, so you do not measure anything and just drop it in the drum, and the film dissolves in water and releases the detergent. With liquid detergent or powder you pour out the amount yourself, which makes it harder to get the standard dose right, but the unit price is usually lower. The cleaning principle is similar, and the main differences are convenience and the cost of each wash, so choose by your habits and budget.
What do numbers such as "6X" and "3.9 times concentrated" mean?
These multiples are mostly relative comparisons from the maker's own tests or marketing (for example against its own older product or a regular detergent), not a common third-party verified standard, and you cannot compare the numbers directly between brands. You can use them as a reference, but it is better to look at the actual ingredient list, whether there is a third-party certification, and the cost per pod after you calculate it.
Can I put laundry pods in the detergent drawer of the washing machine?
Most brands tell you to put the pod at the bottom of the drum and then add the clothes, and not in the detergent drawer. The drawer is designed for liquid or powder detergent, and a capsule may not dissolve completely there, which reduces the cleaning. Follow the instructions on the package, and a quick-wash mode is usually not recommended either.
